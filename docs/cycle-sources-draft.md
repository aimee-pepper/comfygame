# Cycle — a source list (draft for cutting)

**Why this exists.** Per-target exclusivity exposed a problem: **Cycle had no primary source of its own.** Period came from how celestials move, amplitude was derived from Illumination and Thermal swing, regularity came from Constancy qualifiers on *other* targets' sources. Under "one primary per target," Cycle's primary would have been the sun — already Illumination's primary.

Only two sources touch Cycle today, and both incidentally: **Comet** and **Rift**.

**The fix (Aimee, session 13): give Cycle real primaries.** Things that dictate how a world keeps time, rather than inheriting it from what's in the sky. The sun stays Illumination's primary; celestial Length and Phase qualifiers still modify *how* it moves within whatever rhythm the world has.

This makes Cycle more interesting than it was rather than merely rescuing it.

---

## What Cycle resolves to (unchanged)

- **Period** — how long a full turn of the world takes
- **Amplitude** — how much conditions swing across it
- **Regularity** — erratic ↔ metronomic
- Tags: *seasonal*, *tidal*, *arrhythmic*, *frozen-in-time*

## Candidate primaries — cut freely

Grouped by what kind of world they imply. **[ALL PLACEHOLDER]**

### Worlds that keep time reliably

| Source | What it is | Period | Amplitude | Regularity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Tide** | The world moves to a pull. Everything rises and falls with it. | mid | **high** | **high** |
| **Orrery** | A great clockwork, still running. Whoever built it is long gone. | mid | low | **very high** |
| **Pulse** | The world itself beats, slowly. You feel it through the ground. | short | mid | high |
| **Procession** | Things arrive and depart in fixed order. Seasons as a parade. | **long** | high | high |

### Worlds that keep time badly, or not at all

| Source | What it is | Period | Amplitude | Regularity |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Stillness** | Nothing turns. No day, no season, no change. | — | **none** | n/a |
| **Drift** | Time passes, but not evenly. Some spans stretch. | long | mid | **low** |
| **Stutter** | The world repeats itself slightly. Things happen twice. | short | low | **very low** |
| **Unwinding** | The rhythm is running down. It used to be regular. | lengthening | falling | falling |

### Worlds whose time is strange

| Source | What it is | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| **Breath** | Long slow inhale and exhale. Everything sleeps, then wakes. | Very long period, very high amplitude — a world with two utterly different states |
| **Cascade** | Change arrives all at once, then nothing for a long time. | Low regularity, extreme amplitude; the punctuated-equilibrium world |
| **Echo** | The world's rhythm is a repeat of something else's. | Ties to the Strange source family; possibly a rare/late rune |

## Notes

- **Stillness is the standout.** A world where nothing ever turns is genuinely evocative, and it interacts with everything: no day/night niches, no seasonal morphs, specialists rather than generalists, and — per the pressure model — buffered worlds breed fragile things. It's also the natural contradiction partner for any cyclic celestial ("a sun that never moves in a world that never turns" versus "a sun that rises in a world where nothing turns").
- **Tide and Orrery are the safe, useful two** if the list needs to be small. One natural, one built.
- **Unwinding** implies a world in decline, which suits the sundering fiction — possibly a signal that a world was once anchored and isn't any more.
- **Orrery is a site as much as a source** — a world with a clockwork in it should probably *have* the clockwork somewhere, findable. Worth linking to `sites-system.md`.
