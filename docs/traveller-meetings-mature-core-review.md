## Corrin

**Opening**

Corrin has a broad strap looped around a smooth stone. She pulls it through, marks the place it
catches, then works only that thumb-width of material until the fibres warm and darken. “The strap
is fine,” she says. “The shoulder that has to meet it eight hundred times is less theoretical.”

| Stable ID | Ask | Reply |
|---|---|---|
| `corrin.wear_point` | “Why work only that spot?” | “Because comfort fails locally.” She folds the strap where it rubbed. “A material can be strong everywhere and cruel in one repeated place.” |
| `corrin.source_marks` | “What was it made from?” | Corrin turns the clean face down and shows you three faint grain changes. “Three sources, finished until they looked like none. Easier to sell. Harder to remember which piece should never have carried the same load.” |
| `corrin.nothing_wasted` | “At least none of it was wasted.” | “Waste matters.” She tests the softened section again. “It does not travel backward and make taking justified. Useful is an answer about what happens next.” |

**Offer:** “Come home with me. Make us bindings, capacity goods and protection that expects bodies
to move. I'll bring what I know about each material's source too.”

**Accepted:** “Good. Wear this for ten steps.” Corrin hands you the unfinished strap. “If you tell me
it feels fine after one, I will make you walk nine more.”

**Declined:** “Then leave the strap.” She returns to the worn place. “Finishing something is not the
same as knowing where it belongs.”

## Dagg

**Opening**

Dagg drives a heavy practice head into the ground once. The blow is controlled and unimpressive
until a buried slab shifts half a handspan. He does not strike again. He measures the new footing,
clears a space behind his heel, and waits for his breath to become ordinary.

| Stable ID | Ask | Reply |
|---|---|---|
| `dagg.one_strike` | “Why only one?” | “Because the first changed the ground.” He presses a boot against the lifted edge. “Repeating a good answer after the question changes is only momentum pretending to be judgement.” |
| `dagg.recovery_space` | “What are you measuring now?” | “Where the next choice can begin.” Dagg marks the distance behind him. “A blow includes the place the body must return to afterward.” |
| `dagg.cost_of_waiting` | “What if someone cannot wait for the best moment?” | “Then their waiting is part of my calculation.” His expression tightens. “I learned that after being correct too late and calling the delay patience.” |

**Offer:** “Come with me. My party meets weight that punishes hurry and danger that punishes delay.
Teach us to see commitment, impact and recovery as one action.”

**Accepted:** Dagg shoulders the practice head. “Then we start by moving this slab, not proving I can
move it twice.”

**Declined:** “Do not mistake my staying for certainty.” He checks the shifted ground again. “I am
still deciding what this place can bear.”

## Rook

**Opening**

Rook removes a row of pale distance markers and plants it farther from a narrow crossing. The last
marker is split. Beyond it, something large has stopped short of the crossing but within Rook's
spear reach. “It saw the line,” they say. “It could not know what the line meant. That failure is
mine.”

| Stable ID | Ask | Reply |
|---|---|---|
| `rook.moved_line` | “Why move the markers?” | “A warning must arrive before the consequence.” Rook sights along the new row. “The old distance was clear to someone already trained to fear a spear.” |
| `rook.boundary_owner` | “Is this your boundary?” | “I maintain it. That is not the same as owning every approach it crosses.” They glance toward the only dry passage. “A legible line can still be unjustly placed.” |
| `rook.stopped_creature` | “You could reach it from here.” | “And it cannot reach me.” Rook lowers the spear. “That difference creates time. If I spend the time only on striking, distance has taught me nothing.” |

**Offer:** “Come with me. My party should know where contact becomes inevitable while there is still
room to warn, answer or turn away.”

**Accepted:** Rook snaps the broken marker shorter and plants it where even a low gaze will meet it.
“Then this line remains until the crossing changes—not merely until I grow used to it.”

**Declined:** “You have not crossed.” They step aside from the passage. “A boundary that permits only
agreement is not offering a choice.”

## Lys

**Opening**

Lys has laid two weathered records beside each other. Their margins name the same three places, but
between the second and third she has left a clean rectangle of bare ground. “Nothing was lost from
either page,” she says. “Only the account of how one became the other.”

| Stable ID | Ask | Reply |
|---|---|---|
| `lys.missing_relation` | “What belongs in the empty space?” | “A relationship, not necessarily another object.” Lys aligns the edges without closing the gap. “Sequence, contradiction, a person who chose not to be recorded. An archive loses these while every page remains present.” |
| `lys.two_accounts` | “Which record is right?” | “They agree too neatly where neither writer was present and differ where both were.” She taps the repeated line. “Agreement is evidence. So is the route by which agreement became convenient.” |
| `lys.living_subject` | “Can you preserve the full context?” | “No.” Lys does not soften the answer. “Context limits misuse; it does not return control to someone whose words outlived their chance to correct me.” |

**Offer:** “Come home with me. My Library is collecting fragments. Make their relationships
searchable without pretending the gaps are solved, and keep the disagreements visible too.”

**Accepted:** Lys wraps the two records separately. “Good. They travel together, but neither becomes
the other's correction.”

**Declined:** “Then remember the space.” She leaves the bare rectangle undisturbed. “An uncatalogued
absence is still easier to notice than a confident invention.”

## Bracken

**Opening**

Bracken is cutting apart a breastplate that looks almost untouched. He asks you to hold an edge,
then moves your hand away from a buckle hidden underneath. The inner padding has been crushed to a
hard crescent. “The outside retained its shape,” he says. “The wearer was less fortunate.”

| Stable ID | Ask | Reply |
|---|---|---|
| `bracken.intact_outside` | “Why ruin armour that still works?” | “It does not still work.” Bracken lifts the flattened lining. “It completed one instruction—keep the point outside—and transferred every unnamed consequence inward.” |
| `bracken.wearer` | “Who wore it?” | “Someone who twisted left after the blow, breathed shallowly and kept the lower strap too tight.” He studies the marks. “That is what the object proves. A name would be something I added.” |
| `bracken.impenetrable` | “Can you make something nothing gets through?” | “Briefly.” He folds the plate until the hidden buckle catches. “If the wearer agrees not to turn, breathe well, shed heat or leave quickly.” |

**Offer:** “Come home with me. Halloway's forge makes strong work. Build what comes after it: armour
chosen for the expected harm and the cost a particular wearer can carry.”

**Accepted:** “Is Halloway still there?” At your answer, Bracken gathers the separated layers. “Good.
She will object precisely enough to be useful.”

**Declined:** “Then do not put this plate back together.” He sets the intact outer shell aside. “A
convincing surface is how the same failure gets another wearer.”

## Fen

**Opening**

Fen has tied six cords between two stones. None belongs to a weapon. They pluck one, wait while the
wind passes through it, then cut it down. “It returned almost to the same note,” they say. “Almost is
where the hundredth draw lives.”

| Stable ID | Ask | Reply |
|---|---|---|
| `fen.hundred_draws` | “Are you making a bow?” | “Not yet. I am finding which material remains itself after being asked the same question a hundred times.” Fen coils the rejected cord instead of discarding it. “This one may prefer a sling.” |
| `fen.path_before_target` | “What are you aiming at?” | “Nothing.” They watch loose fibres lift in the crosswind. “Choosing a target before understanding the path is how people blame weather for work they never prepared.” |
| `fen.weapon_and_hunt` | “Did you hunt with what you made?” | “Sometimes. Making a path possible and deciding what should be at its end are different jobs.” Fen stills the next cord. “They remain different when one person performs both.” |

**Offer:** “Come with me. I have materials worth testing and journeys where distance matters. Make
us bows, launchers and thrown weapons that account for the whole physical path.”

**Accepted:** Fen cuts down the remaining cords in order and labels each coil. “Then they come too.
Rejected for one use is not the same as useless.”

**Declined:** “All right.” They pluck the next cord and listen past its first note. “A path may be
understood before anyone is obliged to travel it.”

## Batch review checks

- **Practice separation:** Corrin reads repeated body contact; Dagg reads commitment/recovery; Rook
  maintains perceivable approach; Lys arranges relationships among records; Bracken reads transferred
  harm; Fen prepares physical trajectory.
- **Station alignment:** Tannery, Library, Armoury and Bowyer offers name their distinct promises
  without duplicating Blacksmith, Trading Post or Channelworks. Dagg and Rook offer combat literacy only.
- **Ethical tension remains playable:** the draft does not demand that the player endorse any
  traveller's conclusion, and no reply turns a contradiction into a hidden morality quiz.
- **No mechanical leakage:** dialogue does not name diary-exclusive focuses, thresholds, component
  IDs, exact recipes or unrevealed signature facts.
- **Pronoun/name state:** Bracken and Fen retain the currently documented proposed pronouns; review
  may revise copy and pronouns together without changing stable exchange identity.

### Design-lead revision log — 11 Aug 2026

- Rook's older opening placed the creature “without entering spear reach,” while the independent
  reply `rook.stopped_creature` immediately said Rook could reach it. The current staging puts the
  creature short of the crossing but inside Rook's reach, preserving the ethical asymmetry and making
  the optional exchange coherent in every order.
- All six older offers drifted into the traveller's voice despite occupying the player's recruit
  button. Current offers are player invitations; terminal lines remain traveller responses.
