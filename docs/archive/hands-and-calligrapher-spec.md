# The Hands Are Too Cheap — the Calligrapher

**Status:** from Aimee's brief, 6 Aug. Numbers **[PLACEHOLDER]**.

> *"upgrading to pencil (and the penmanship tree in general) is WAY too cheap. Those are massively game changing upgrades and need to be gated behind expensive and rare materials as well as the calligrapher's shop, and then the last upgrade gated behind a calligrapher shop upgrade."*

---

## 1. How badly they're underpriced

| Upgrade | Current cost | What it does |
|---|---|---|
| **A pencil** | 30 essence · 6 fibre | Footprints 4–6 cells → **2–3**. Roughly **doubles** what a page can say |
| **A fountain pen** | 90 essence · 20 ore · 12 fibre | Footprints → **1×1**. **4–6× crude capacity** |
| **Chaining** | 70 essence · 14 ore · 1 mote | Lifts one-primary-per-target across the whole vocabulary |

On a 6×6 page: **charcoal fits about 7 sigils, pencil about 14, fountain pen 36.** The pencil is the largest capability jump in the game and costs less than a storehouse tier. Common materials only — nothing rare anywhere in the branch.

## 2. The Calligrapher

A new trade, and therefore a new class (twelfth) and a new building.

| Class | Stat lean | In a fight | Unlocks |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Calligrapher** | Focus | Back rank; support | **The Scriptorium** — the Penmanship tree, hands, compound assembly |

Per Q40, the building owns the branch: **Penmanship moves off the Workshop and into the Scriptorium.** And per the tier rule, **the fountain pen requires the Scriptorium at tier 2** — Aimee's "last upgrade gated behind a shop upgrade."

## 3. The Calligrapher is REQUIRED — and that's the point (Aimee)

> *"the player MUST meet the calligrapher to progress. it's core to the game."*

**Everything Penmanship lives at the Scriptorium. No Workshop fallback, no first-rung-free rule.** Option 3 of the three I offered, chosen deliberately.

**This is a stated exception to Q40's gating rule** (*a building's tree has its first rungs available from the start, so finding the person accelerates rather than unblocks*). That rule protects **capacity** progression — nobody should be stuck at 16 storehouse slots because a tanner hasn't turned up. **It does not apply to the hands**, because the hands aren't a convenience the game withholds — they are the thing the game is *about*.

**Why it's right, rather than merely allowed:** the game's premise is learning to write. A Binder who can only scrawl in charcoal is not being blocked by a missing shopkeeper — they haven't yet found the person who teaches the Art. Meeting the Calligrapher is a **story beat that happens to be a gate**, which is what a required character should be.

It also makes the constraint honest. On a 6×6 page charcoal fits about seven sigils, and a cluster is a target plus a source plus a qualifier, so charcoal buys **about two clusters**. A six-condition signature is *unwritable* in charcoal. That isn't a balance problem to be smoothed over — it's the reason the Calligrapher matters.

### What this requires of the Calligrapher

Since they're mandatory, they can't be a lottery:

- **[PROPOSAL] Their signature must be writable in charcoal.** Two or three conditions, common vocabulary. A required character behind a signature you need the pencil to reach is a deadlock.
- **[PROPOSAL] Their pages should be weighted early**, so the trail toward them surfaces before a player has exhausted what charcoal can do.
- **[PROPOSAL] They should be findable more than one way** — their own diary, and a mention in someone else's — so a single unlucky page distribution can't hide them.

**A required gate needs a guaranteed key.** None of that softens the gate; it just makes sure the gate has a road to it.

## 4. Repricing — [PLACEHOLDER]

The principle: **each hand should cost a world you had to deliberately write**, not accumulated pocket change.

| Upgrade | Proposed cost | Why |
|---|---|---|
| **A pencil** | ~150 essence · timber · **lead** | Lead and wood is literally what an early pencil was. Lead is a ductile mineral — you must write a metal-bearing world |
| **A fountain pen** | ~500 essence · resin · **silver** · **quartz** | A fine nib and a barrel. Silver is high-richness ductile; quartz is hard and rich. Requires **Scriptorium tier 2** |
| **Chaining** | ~350 essence · **mercury** · motes | Mercury is rare, ductile-and-volatile, and thematically the right thing for a rune that lets separate things join |
| **Compound assembly** | Scriptorium tier 2 · **adamant** | The endgame of the writing progression should want the endgame material |

**Every one of these names a rare material from a specific kind of world**, so upgrading your hand means *going and writing for it* — which is the loop the whole game is built on, applied to the tool you use to play it.

## 5. Knock-ons

- **Twelve classes now.** Worth checking that's still distinct rather than a list.
- **Penmanship leaves the Workshop.** The Workshop keeps Instruction and The Hand — still "the place you get better at writing," just at the level below the instruments.
- **The Scriptorium is a strong argument for Q40**: a building whose tree *is* the thing it's for is exactly what per-building trees were meant to produce.
- **Balance check needed after:** with the hands both costlier and gated, early-game page space becomes the binding constraint for considerably longer. That is now the intended shape — the crude hand *should* feel like a real limitation — but how long that stretch lasts wants playtesting rather than arithmetic.
- **The tutorial question sharpens.** If meeting the Calligrapher is required, the trail to them is effectively the game's opening arc, and it's the natural place for a tutorial to live.
