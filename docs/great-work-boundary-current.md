# Current Design Boundary — The Atlas and Great Work

**Status:** Settled premise and implementation-safe data boundary. Ending structure remains held by
explicit player direction in `archive/decisions-session-9.md`; this document does not propose it.

## Settled and usable now

- The old Atlas anchored the known realms together. Its theft and destruction caused the Sundering.
- Rebuilding the Atlas **is** the Great Work.
- A unit of restoration is a realm re-anchored, not a crafted component assembled on a parallel track.
- Named places partly persist because they belonged to old Atlas pages; travellers and histories were
  scattered when the binding broke.
- Anchoring is literal rebinding. The three current routes produce the same durable realm state.
- Opposition may obstruct restoration in progress or make an un-restored place harder to reach.
- **Finality is a pillar:** no cult action, reset, ending or systemic event may un-anchor a completed
  realm, revoke recovered knowledge, remove a recruited traveller, or reverse completed base progress.

## Safe data to preserve before an ending rule exists

For each anchored realm, retain:

- stable realm ID, seed and authored page/signature fingerprint;
- anchor route and date/order as history only;
- active/dormant state, sustain history and revisit state;
- discovered named places, sites and unique depletion state;
- traveller encounters, diary pages and other authored relationships associated with it;
- world readings and the source vocabulary that produced them.

This data allows a later Great Work rule to evaluate meaningful restoration without deciding that rule
now. Do **not** expose an ending-facing score, quota, quality grade or “valid Atlas page” badge.

## Explicitly unresolved — no placeholder outcome

- why the cult stole the Atlas, whether the Sundering was intended, and whether surviving accounts agree;
- what, if anything, is coming;
- whether the Great Work culminates in completion, confrontation, choice, or another structure;
- how many realms are required and whether every anchored realm contributes equally;
- whether a Reality reset remains in the campaign at all;
- if a reset exists, its trigger, player purpose, reward and relationship to the finality rule;
- Tam's unique act and any relationship between that act and Glass.

These are review questions for Aimee, not implementation placeholders. Engineering may preserve data
but must not infer an ending from it.

## Reality reset hard boundary

No current feature should assume a reset. If one is later retained, it cannot mean erasing completion
and replaying the same campaign from zero. At minimum, the settled finality rule protects:

- anchored realms and their histories;
- recruited travellers and animals;
- diary, bestiary and instrument knowledge;
- base/station construction and permanent hand progression.

This list constrains a future design; it does not assert that reset exists or decide what it changes.

## Tam re-entry gate

Tam remains traveller 29 and receives no focus, signature, diary, meeting or engineering placeholder.
Design resumes only when Aimee chooses enough of the unresolved endgame structure to reveal one
genuinely missing player action. Tam's ordinary former work must plausibly supply half of that action;
the Sundering supplies the impossible half. Their importance may create responsibility, never proof
that they were secretly exceptional.

## Current review packet for Aimee

When Aimee is ready, discuss these as one related batch rather than isolated yes/no questions:

1. What emotional act should complete a long restoration campaign: joining, repairing, choosing,
   refusing, witnessing, or something else?
2. Is “Reality reset” still desired after permanent anchored realms exist? If yes, what new play does
   it create that ordinary continued play cannot?
3. Should Atlas progress value breadth, particular restored relationships/places, or simply the
   player's chosen collection? No count should be chosen before this meaning is chosen.
4. Only then: what endgame act remains impossible for everyone else, and what ordinary work taught Tam
   the part of it that is not magic?
