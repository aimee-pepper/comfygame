# The Roster — 28 named travellers

**Companion to `the-cast.md`**, which holds the structure. This holds the people.

**Names and one-line voices are proposals.** They're the most overrulable thing here — I've matched the register of the six that exist (Mara, Edren, Halloway, Isolde, Sela, Tovin): short, slightly archaic, no fantasy apostrophes.

**Every one is recruitable.** No traveller is met-only.

## 3. Isolde's diary rune — **Stillness**

**Not a light word.** My *Gleam* proposal was *"constant illumination like Crystal but cheaper in greed"* — and Aimee's objection kills it: **with the greed fix, Sun is both stronger and cheap, so there is no cheap-light niche.** I invented a gap the greed fix had already closed.

**Her own original clue had the answer in it:** *"The air is thin and terribly still. Nothing here disturbs a page."*

| | |
|---|---|
| **Rune** | **Stillness** — Atmosphere |
| **Does** | **Lowers atmospheric motion.** The only focus that does |
| **Why hers** | A calligrapher needs air that doesn't move, not light |
| **Why it has purpose** | It's a **capability rune**, not a weaker version of anything |

**Knock-on:** the Cycle focus also called *Stillness* ("nothing turns") needs another name — **[PROPOSAL] Hush**.

## 4. The sixteenth — **Lys, the archivist** (Aimee)

**An archivist is not an archaeologist**, and I had conflated them. Edren digs things up; an archivist keeps and reads what's been kept. **They're two people and two buildings.**

| | **Edren** — archaeologist | **Lys** — archivist |
|---|---|---|
| **Building** | **The Reliquary** | **The Library** |
| **About** | **Sites.** Finding ruins, reading them, richer rewards from them | **Pages.** Diaries, hint pages, rune study, cross-referencing |
| **Diary rune** | **Ruin** | **Echo** — the world repeating something, which is what a record *is* |
| **Phase** | opening · 2 | mid · 4 |

**This is a better split than one person doing both.** The Library is the spine of the search loop — hint pages, clue cross-referencing, the *"you don't have the words for this"* marker — and it deserves someone whose whole trade is reading. Edren's Reliquary is about the *field*: where ruins are, what's in them, what the people who came before left.

**And it makes Edren's early placement make sense.** An archaeologist you meet in the opening points you at ruins, which is where diary pages live — so finding him early feeds the search loop before you have a Library to read in.

### Withdrawn: Reed the papermaker

I proposed her to give **Pulp** a purpose. Aimee: *"do not backfill to make things make sense."* That's precisely what it was — a resource with no use, so I built a person, a building and a mechanic around it rather than asking whether the resource should exist.

**Page quality was separately bad** — a second randomness axis on a page that already rolls whatever you don't write.

**Paper and Pulp may not need to exist**, since essence already binds books. **[AIMEE]** floated keeping paper for **anchor books** specifically, which is a real use for a real thing.

### Fighters and the combat trees

**The per-fighter branches I proposed are replaced by three shared trees** — see `combat-trees.md`. Offense, Defense and Craft, three branches each, and a class is *where you spent* rather than what you were given.

So the **Branch teaches** column above should be read as **a starting lean**, not a private tree. Bryn begins in Protection; nothing stops you taking her into Force.

**What still makes a fighter worth recruiting**, given they build nothing — **[AIMEE]**, my lean is the first and third:
1. **Better starting leans and faster growth** in combat branches
2. A unique skill nobody else can learn
3. **An exclusive gambit component** — the column above

## 6a. Staffing — posted or in the party

**Every trade companion above is worth having in two different places**, and they're mutually exclusive:

- **Posted to their building** → a **discount** on everything it does
- **In your party** → **the building rises alongside them**, slowly, as they level

**Their earning is never taxed.** They level exactly as they would anywhere; the building benefits from their growth without taking anything from it. And **paying for a tier outright is always available** — the keeper's level is an additional slow track, never a requirement.

**Orsa is the exception in shape:** the **Tavern is the upgraded Firepit**, so she doesn't add a building — she raises the one you started with.

Full system in `building-staffing.md`.

## 7. Random companions (Aimee)

> *"random companions are the ones you can find out in the world that are a random class and you can recruit them before finding the diary characters. they can also be assigned to anchored worlds. they should spawn more on worlds with sites or greed or lots of life."*

**They're real, and they arrive first.** Before you've deduced anybody's signature, before you know there are people to look for, you stumble on someone in a world you wrote for other reasons.

| | |
|---|---|
| **When** | **From the start** — findable before any named traveller |
| **Class** | Random, from the combat trees — a starting lean, no calling |
| **Cost** | **A want** — bring, find, show or reach something |
| **Diary** | **None.** No pages, no hint page, no exclusive rune |
| **Building** | **None** |
| **Postings** | Party · home stations · **assigned to anchored worlds** |

### Where they spawn — [PROPOSAL]

More likely in worlds with **sites**, **greed**, or **lots of life**. Which reads true: people are found where there's something worth being near.

- **Sites** — someone sheltering in a ruin, camped by a landmark
- **Greed** — rich worlds draw people, and that gives writing a greedy world a *second* reason beyond loot
- **Vitality** — a world that can feed someone is a world someone stays in

**Nice consequence:** the same conditions that make a world dangerous and valuable also make it populated. **Greed buys you people as well as ore**, which is a good reason to write one beyond the haul.

### The line between them and the cast

**Named travellers have diaries. Random companions don't.** A diary is the entire search apparatus — pages, clues, a hint page, an exclusive rune, cross-references to others.

**Random companions are people you meet. Named ones are people you look for.** They fill the party and the postings; the named cast carries the buildings, the vocabulary and the story.
