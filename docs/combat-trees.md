# Combat Trees

> *"why are you trying to redesign the wheel. come up with 3 combat trees with 3 branches each and that can create a myriad of classes. defense, magic, and offense maybe. so a rogue would go evasion in the defense tree, poison or stealth in the magic tree, and speed in the attack tree for example."*

**Replaces** the per-companion combat branches I proposed in `cast-roster.md` §5 — nine bespoke trees where three shared ones do the job better.

---

## 1. The structure

**Three trees. Three branches each. Nine branches total, shared by everyone.**

A character isn't *given* a class — **a class is where they've spent.** A rogue is Evasion + Shadow + Swiftness. A knight is Fortitude + Protection + Force. Nobody authored either; both fall out of nine branches.

| Tree | Branch | Branch | Branch |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Offense** | **Force** | **Precision** | **Swiftness** |
| **Defense** | **Fortitude** | **Evasion** | **Protection** |
| **Craft** | **Venom** | **Shadow** | **Kindling** |

**"Craft" rather than "magic"** — this world has no magic. It has the Art, alchemy, and emanation. Craft covers all three without implying spellcasting, and it's what the Apothecary and the Emanant actually do.

## 2. The nine branches

### OFFENSE

**Force** — crush damage · stagger · heavy weapons · damage through armour
*Answers:* armoured foes. *Costs:* initiative.
Skills: **Overbear** · stagger · shatter

**Precision** — pierce damage · finding gaps · finishing wounded foes
*Answers:* plated and high-armour foes. *Costs:* raw damage.
Skills: **Pry** · exploit · finish

**Swiftness** — initiative · extra actions · multiple targets
*Answers:* swarms, and anything you want to kill before it acts.
Skills: **Quicken** · flurry · first strike

### DEFENSE

**Fortitude** — max HP · armour value · status resistance
*Answers:* attrition. *Costs:* speed.
Skills: brace · endure · **Ward**

**Evasion** — dodge · initiative · back-rank mobility
*Answers:* single heavy hitters. *Costs:* toughness.
Skills: sidestep · **Fall Back** · slip

**Protection** — guarding others · drawing attacks · sharing damage
*Answers:* protecting a fragile back rank.
Skills: **Draw Off** · cover · shield-another

### CRAFT

**Venom** — toxins · damage over turns · weapon coatings
*Answers:* high-HP foes, and anything armour would blunt.
Skills: envenom · **Flense** · corrode

**Shadow** — concealment · ambush · avoiding encounters entirely
*Answers:* fights you'd rather not have; opening a fight on your terms.
Skills: conceal · ambush · **Snuff**

**Kindling** — elemental attacks · burn, freeze, shock · resistances
*Answers:* warded and elemental foes.
Skills: elemental strikes · **Steady** · quench

## 3. How classes emerge

Nobody is assigned a class. **A calling is a starting lean and a name; where they end up is the player's.**

| Build | Emerges as |
|---|---|
| Evasion + Shadow + Swiftness | **Rogue** *(Aimee's example)* |
| Fortitude + Protection + Force | **Knight** |
| Precision + Venom + Swiftness | **Hunter** |
| Fortitude + Kindling + Protection | **Warden** |
| Force + Fortitude + Venom | **Brute** |
| Precision + Shadow + Evasion | **Assassin** |
| Kindling + Venom + Precision | **Alchemist** |
| Protection + Kindling + Fortitude | **Bulwark** |
| Swiftness + Shadow + Evasion | **Skirmisher** |

**Nine branches make far more than nine classes**, and two players' Hunters won't be the same Hunter.

## 4. How you spend in them

**[PROPOSAL]** points from **levelling** (session 17), spent freely across all nine. **No class restrictions** — a smith can learn Shadow if you want a smith who does that.

**A companion's calling gives a starting lean, not a limit.** Halloway begins with a point or two in Force; nothing stops you making her a knife-fighter.

**[PROPOSAL] Deeper nodes cost more**, so spreading across nine branches is broad and shallow, and committing to three is deep. **That's the real decision** — and with a party of five it's a party-wide one: five generalists play very differently from three specialists and two supports.

## 5. What this replaces and what it fixes

**Replaces:** nine bespoke per-companion branches. Those needed nine trees authored, made every companion a fixed archetype, and meant benching someone lost their tree entirely.

**Fixes four things at once:**
- **Nine branches instead of nine trees** — a third of the authoring, and it never grows with the cast.
- **Classes emerge instead of being assigned**, which is what session 15 established for creatures and should be true of people too.
- **The twelve classes in `companions-classes-spec-v2.md` become descriptions rather than definitions** — Warden and Hunter are *builds*, and the spec's class list can shrink to trades.
- **The skills that already exist have homes.** Pry, Overbear, Flense, Ward, Draw Off, Quicken, Snuff, Steady and Fall Back all slot into a branch. Nothing is orphaned, and the gaps show where new skills are needed.

## 6. What still separates trades from fighters

Trade companions unlock **buildings**; every companion uses **the same nine branches**. So a smith and a warden differ by *what they let you build*, not by *how they can fight* — which means recruiting for a building never costs you a fighter.

**Fighter companions have no building, so their reason to exist has to be something else — [AIMEE]:**
1. **Better starting leans and faster growth** in combat branches
2. **A unique skill** nobody else can learn
3. **An exclusive gambit component** — the shape I proposed in `cast-roster.md` §5

*My lean is 1 and 3: fighters start further along and bring a way of thinking, rather than a locked technique.*

## 7. Max level is derived from these trees (Aimee)

> *"we'll have to determine max levels after determining the combat trees. we want all companions to be able to max out 3 trees."* — clarified: ***"they need to reach the end of each tree on any one branch per tree."***

**Settled: one branch to the end, in each of the three trees.**

So a fully-levelled companion has **three completed branches** — one from Offense, one from Defense, one from Craft — and **six they never touched**.

| | |
|---|---|
| **Max level** | Enough points to complete **exactly three branches**, one per tree |
| **A finished companion** | 3 of 9 branches, at full depth |
| **Aimee's rogue** | Swiftness *(Offense)* · Evasion *(Defense)* · Shadow *(Craft)* — complete, and permanently not a knight |

### Why this is the right shape

**Builds stay distinct forever.** There is no convergence point where every companion is the same — a rogue can never also be a knight, and that holds at maximum level with a party of five and a roster of twenty-eight.

**Every companion is a real choice, not an order-of-operations puzzle.** With nine branches and three slots there are **27 combinations**, before counting *how far* you'd gone in each along the way.

**And it makes the party composition question permanent.** Five companions, three completed branches each, chosen from nine — you cannot cover everything, so who you bring stays a decision at every point in the game.

**[PROPOSAL] Partial investment should be possible en route.** Nothing should force you to finish a branch before touching another; the cap is on *total points*, so spreading them means three unfinished branches rather than three finished ones. **The commitment is the interesting part** — and a player who spreads has made a real, slightly worse choice rather than an illegal one.

## 8. Wild companions arrive already built (Aimee)

Found at **the player's current level**, with their points **already spent** — otherwise a level-14 find who arrives at level 1 is worthless, and late-game recruiting would be pointless.

**[PROPOSAL] spent coherently**, following a hidden lean, so a wild companion arrives as a recognisable build — *you meet a skirmisher, not a bag of points.*

**[AIMEE]** whether they can be respecced. *(Leaning yes: otherwise a wild companion is a lottery on someone else's choices.)*

## 9. Open

1. **Branch depth** — how many nodes each. **This decides the level cap**: max level = enough points for three complete branches.
2. **Point income** per level, and whether respec exists.
3. **Whether animals use these trees** or a simpler one.
4. **Whether the branch names are right** — Kindling especially. *(Craft's third branch is elemental, and "kindling" leans fiery when it also covers freezing and shock.)*
