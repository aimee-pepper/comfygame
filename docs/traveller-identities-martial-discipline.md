# Traveller Identity Drafts — Talin, Dagg, Rook and Marrick

**Status:** Identity and diary directions approved; Dagg's teaching is now specified  
**Scope:** Four dedicated fighters whose combat functions already exist but whose human premises did
not

> **Signature authority:** Talin's current three-condition profile is in
> `traveller-signatures-early-mid-current.md`. Dagg and Rook's four-condition profiles are in
> `traveller-signatures-mid-current.md`; Marrick's six-condition profile is in
> `traveller-signature-marrick-current.md`.

These travellers are separated by how they understand **time and commitment in a fight**. Talin
acts before the opening closes. Dagg refuses to spend force before it will matter. Rook makes distance
into a boundary. Marrick makes repeated coordination into something other people can trust.

## Discipline contrast

| | **Talin** | **Dagg** | **Rook** | **Marrick** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Combat moment | The opening | The recovery | The approach | The long duration |
| Discipline | Decide in time | Commit weight deliberately | Make distance legible | Make coordination repeatable |
| Failure feared | A correct decision made too late | Force spent because urgency demanded a performance | A warning nobody could understand | A plan that protects only those already inside it |
| Exclusive gambit | Foe: armour > x | **Self: recovery complete** | Foe: cannot reach me | Ally: HP < x, any |

# Talin — Blade

## Identity

| Field | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Pronouns** | she/her |
| **Calling** | a blade fighter trained to exploit brief structural openings |
| **Campaign phase** | Mid |
| **Signature complexity** | 3 conditions |
| **Teaching** | Exclusive gambit property: **Foe: armour > x** |
| **Starting lean** | Precision + Swiftness, with Pierce access |

### One-line blurb

> Makes decisions quickly and judges them slowly. Knows hesitation is still a choice somebody pays
> for.

### Voice anchors

- **Habit:** Notices closures: a shifting plate settling back into place, a route becoming impassable,
  someone reaching the end of a sentence where an answer must begin.
- **Attitude:** Decisive without swagger. Talin rarely celebrates acting first; speed is an obligation
  to accept incomplete information, not proof she is cleverer than everyone else.
- **Tension:** She has saved people by deciding before certainty arrived. She has also hurt people by
  mistaking the need for a decision for evidence that hers was correct.

Talin should not be restless or sharp-tongued merely because she has high initiative. She can be
quite still. What distinguishes her is a practiced willingness to become responsible for a choice
while there is still time for that choice to matter.

## Why the player seeks her

- a strong Pierce and initiative starting lean;
- techniques for opening or finishing against plated foes;
- the armour-threshold gambit property, allowing tactics to change when ordinary attacks will be
  inefficient;
- a fighter who rewards early commitment and target priority rather than raw turn frequency.

### Coherence sentence

> Because Talin learned to **recognise the instant a protected structure becomes vulnerable**, she
> teaches the party to act on **armour thresholds** and exploit openings before they close.

## Signature and diary shape

Her three-condition world should contain recurring narrow windows: a route exposed only during one
cycle state, plated life whose protection opens during a behaviour, and terrain that rewards choosing
a direction before complete information is available.

**Initial target: 7 pages**

| Pages | Purpose |
|---:|---|
| 3 | One location page per signature condition |
| 1 | Armour-threshold gambit teaching |
| 1 | A decision that was right for reasons Talin did not actually know |
| 1 | Dagg or Maud relationship page |
| 1 | Closed route, pierced structure or Talin whereabouts page |

### Diary arc

- **First impression:** Talin appears to possess unusually reliable judgement.
- **Complication:** Some of her reputation rests on outcomes that made hurried reasoning look wiser
  than it was.
- **After recruitment:** She remains decisive, but becomes more willing to name the uncertainty she
  accepted instead of letting success rewrite it as certainty.

## Relationship anchors

- **Dagg:** Acting before the opening closes versus refusing to be hurried into a weak commitment.
- **Maud:** A fitted blade can support judgement but cannot make the decision on the wielder's behalf.
- **Bryn:** Intervention must sometimes happen before the endangered person can ask for it; both care
  about who bears the consequence of that call.

# Dagg — Hammer

## Identity

| Field | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Pronouns** | he/him |
| **Calling** | a hammer fighter who studies recovery, footing and committed weight |
| **Campaign phase** | Mid |
| **Signature complexity** | 4 conditions |
| **Teaching** | Recovery-oriented gambit component **TBD after active-skill design** |
| **Starting lean** | Force + Fortitude, with stagger access |

### One-line blurb

> Refuses to hurry a blow merely because somebody is frightened by the pause before it.

### Voice anchors

- **Habit:** Tests footing and recovery space before weight. He watches what a person does immediately
  after exertion, when practiced control is hardest to imitate.
- **Attitude:** Deliberate, articulate and intolerant of urgency used as theatre. He is not slow to
  understand; he is unwilling to substitute visible effort for effective force.
- **Tension:** Patience protects Dagg from wasteful commitments, but waiting for the strongest moment
  can leave somebody else enduring the weaker moments alone.

His mastery is not “being very strong.” It is knowing that a heavy action includes preparation,
impact and recovery—and arranging all three so another action remains possible afterward.

## Why the player seeks him

- strong crush, stagger and Force access against armoured enemies;
- techniques whose payoff depends on recovery rhythm rather than constant action;
- **Self: recovery complete**, an exclusive gambit subject that fires on the first actionable turn
  after all skipped-turn recovery is paid; see `dagg-recovery-teaching-current.md`;
- a deliberate heavy fighter who does not duplicate Halloway's craft knowledge.

Halloway strikes a material held in a chosen position and listens for how it changes. Dagg strikes an
opponent that observes him back, moves, deceives and exploits recovery. Their skills rhyme; they are
not interchangeable.

### Coherence sentence

> Because Dagg understands **force as a cycle of footing, commitment and recovery**, he teaches the
> party to reason about **commitment and recovery** instead of treating every turn as an identical
> opportunity. His gambit language acts after recovery because a skipped recovery turn cannot run a
> gambit at all.

## Signature and diary shape

His four conditions should create alternating pressure and release: ground that settles after impact,
slow heavy creatures with readable recovery, a resource that rewards controlled force, and a route
where rushing repeatedly costs more time than waiting once.

**Initial target: 8 pages**

| Pages | Purpose |
|---:|---|
| 4 | One location page per signature condition |
| 1 | Recovery gambit teaching, exact component TBD |
| 1 | Halloway relationship page: responsive opponent versus cooperative material |
| 1 | Talin disagreement or Maud weapon-fitting page |
| 1 | Failed footing, impact site or Dagg whereabouts page |

### Diary arc

- **First impression:** Dagg appears almost impossible to rush into a mistake.
- **Complication:** He once recognised the best moment correctly but treated the suffering before it
  as somebody else's cost.
- **After recruitment:** He keeps his deliberateness while learning that timing cannot be judged only
  by the eventual strike.

## Relationship anchors

- **Halloway:** Shared material literacy, sharply different conditions of use.
- **Talin:** Their disagreement should sometimes reverse; either speed or patience can become a way
  to avoid naming uncertainty.
- **Maud:** She fits weapons to bodies; Dagg tests whether the body can recover from what the weapon
  invites it to attempt.

# Rook — Spear

## Identity

| Field | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Pronouns** | they/them |
| **Calling** | a spear fighter and keeper of contested approaches |
| **Campaign phase** | Mid |
| **Signature complexity** | 4 conditions |
| **Teaching** | Exclusive gambit subject: **Foe: cannot reach me** |
| **Starting lean** | Precision + Protection, with a reach-opening technique |

### One-line blurb

> Believes a boundary should be visible from both sides. Stands far enough back that approaching it
> remains a choice.

### Voice anchors

- **Habit:** Marks approaches rather than territories: where sight begins, where speech becomes
  possible, where one more step would force an answer.
- **Attitude:** Formal but not rigid, attentive to warnings and whether they can actually be perceived.
  Rook values reach because it can create time to stop, speak or turn away before contact.
- **Tension:** A maintained boundary can protect choice. It can also become a permanent refusal to
  let changed circumstances—or changed people—come closer.

Rook is not a generic guard. Their discipline concerns the space before contact and the obligation to
make consequences legible inside it. A surprise attack may be effective, but it proves nothing about
whether a line was fairly held.

## Why the player seeks them

- control of the opening exchange and attacks across rank differences;
- a strong reach and first-contact lean;
- `Foe: cannot reach me`, letting gambits exploit opponents who cannot answer from their current
  range without asking the player to compare or remember numerical reach values;
- techniques that hold a line without duplicating Marrick's formation buffs or Fen's projectile craft.

Fen designs an object to cross distance. Rook uses distance to delay contact and constrain approach.
The same span means trajectory to one and consent, warning and leverage to the other.

### Coherence sentence

> Because Rook learned to make **approach, warning and consequence occupy visible space**, they teach
> the party to compare **reach** and control the moment before close contact.

## Signature and diary shape

Their four-condition world should be organised around approaches: long but bounded sightlines, narrow
crossings, life that defends distance before attacking, and a changing condition that redraws what
can safely be approached.

**Initial target: 8 pages**

| Pages | Purpose |
|---:|---|
| 4 | One approach or boundary page per signature condition |
| 1 | Reach-comparison gambit teaching |
| 1 | Fen relationship page: crossing distance versus maintaining it |
| 1 | Marrick or Bryn disagreement over who a defended line includes |
| 1 | Unmarked boundary, abandoned crossing or Rook whereabouts page |

### Diary arc

- **First impression:** Rook's boundaries appear unusually fair because they make every warning clear.
- **Complication:** A perfectly legible boundary can still be unjust, especially when the person who
  drew it controls the only safe route.
- **After recruitment:** Rook continues to hold lines but accepts that their placement must remain
  revisable, not merely their enforcement consistent.

## Relationship anchors

- **Fen:** Constructed trajectory versus maintained approach.
- **Marrick:** A line as spatial warning versus a line as one part of coordinated formation.
- **Bryn:** Preventing contact at range versus intervening after danger has already reached someone.

# Marrick — Formation leader

## Identity

| Field | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Pronouns** | he/him |
| **Calling** | a formation leader and keeper of shared routines |
| **Campaign phase** | Late-mid |
| **Signature complexity** | 6 conditions |
| **Teaching** | Exclusive gambit subject: **Ally: HP < x, any** |
| **Starting lean** | Protection + Fortitude, with party-wide support |

### One-line blurb

> Builds routines people can still remember while frightened. Counts who the plan forgot before he
> counts whether it worked.

### Voice anchors

- **Habit:** Repeats positions, fallback routes and signals until others can use them without asking.
  He quietly checks whether instructions still work for the tired, injured or newly arrived.
- **Attitude:** Steady, specific and collaborative. Marrick does not demand obedience as proof of
  discipline; a formation nobody understands is merely one person's plan occupying several bodies.
- **Tension:** Repetition can turn trust into reliable coordination. It can also make exclusion look
  natural: the formation works beautifully for everyone it was designed to notice.

Marrick should not be “the competent soldier.” His human premise is that shared habits can hold when
individual judgement is exhausted—and that this makes whoever designs those habits responsible for
their blind spots.

## Why the player seeks him

- party-wide buffs and formation tools for long fights;
- strong Protection and Fortitude access without replacing Bryn's personal interception;
- `Ally: HP < x, any`, allowing a gambit to respond when anyone in the formation crosses a danger
  threshold rather than requiring a preselected ally;
- coordination that rewards stable party structure and planned fallback behaviour.

Bryn chooses to receive or redirect danger for a particular person. Marrick creates routines that
change what the whole party does before individual rescue becomes necessary. One is intervention;
the other is infrastructure.

### Coherence sentence

> Because Marrick builds **shared routines that remain usable under fear and fatigue**, he teaches the
> party to notice when **any ally** crosses a danger threshold and to coordinate a formation-wide
> response.

## Signature and diary shape

His six conditions should describe a world where no single safe route persists but several partial
routes can support one another: shifting hazards, relay sightlines, defensible pauses, and resources
distributed so success requires coordinated movement rather than one heroic carrier.

**Initial target: 10 pages**

| Pages | Purpose |
|---:|---|
| 6 | One route, position or shared-routine page per signature condition |
| 1 | Any-ally HP gambit teaching |
| 1 | Bryn relationship page: intervention versus infrastructure |
| 1 | Rook disagreement or Sabine care-routine comparison |
| 1 | A formation that succeeded while abandoning someone outside its count |

### Diary arc

- **First impression:** Marrick makes groups safer without asking any one person to be exceptional.
- **Complication:** His most celebrated formation succeeded because its definition of “everyone” did
  not include the people unable to take their assigned positions.
- **After recruitment:** He retains his faith in shared discipline but begins every plan by asking who
  cannot use it and what alternative they control.

## Relationship anchors

- **Bryn:** The core contrast between personal intervention and designed coordination.
- **Rook:** A defensible line may still exclude the person approaching it.
- **Sabine:** Both use routine to create safety; animal consent exposes assumptions in command language.
- **Bracken:** Protective equipment and formations both transfer cost rather than erase it.
