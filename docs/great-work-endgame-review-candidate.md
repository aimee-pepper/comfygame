# Great Work, continuation and Tam — review candidate

**Status:** Game Design recommendation for Aimee; no implementation authority  
**Depends on:** `great-work-boundary-current.md`, `anchoring-system-current.md`  
**Preserves:** anchored realms, people, knowledge, stations and hand progression are final

## Recommendation in one sentence

Complete the Great Work by choosing and joining a small folio of anchored realms the player has
actually lived with; retire destructive Reality reset, then let the completed Atlas open an optional
new volume without deleting the first; Tam alone can hold the final live seam while it is bound.

## Why not a conventional reset

Almost everything worth caring about is already protected by the finality pillar: anchored realms,
travellers, animals, knowledge, stations and writing progression. Resetting only money, ordinary
inventory and assignments would remove chores rather than create a meaningfully new campaign. It
would also punish a long, collection-oriented player immediately after asking them to value what
they restored.

Recommendation: retire **Reality reset** as a player-facing promise. After completing one Atlas
volume, **Open another volume** may create new optional long-horizon discovery goals and harder
world-writing comparisons while preserving the entire existing campaign. It is continuation, not
prestige: no inventory wipe, multiplier bribe, re-recruitment or repeated base construction.

## What makes a realm part of the Great Work

Every anchored realm remains real and valuable. The final binding uses a player-selected **folio**,
not a hidden quality score over the whole portfolio.

A realm becomes eligible for folio selection when all are true:

1. it is anchored by any of the three equal routes;
2. the player has completed at least one later visit after anchoring; and
3. at least one persistent relationship event occurred there after or during anchoring: a traveller
   meeting, named-site resolution, unique writing recovery, first production delivery, or another
   explicitly receipt-bearing world-history event.

This rejects disposable bind-and-forget worlds without grading aesthetics, danger or loot. Dormant
realms remain eligible because dormancy is access/economy state, not narrative invalidation.

Recommended comparison size: **seven selected realms**, exposed as reviewable tuning until a real
campaign reaches it. Seven is large enough to express a long collection and small enough that the
player can remember each choice. Do not require one biome, pressure target, traveller or rarity per
slot. The player's actual relative diversity should remain visible in the selection comparison, but
similar worlds are valid when they reflect similar authorship.

Before commitment, the Atlas shows compact History covers for all eligible realms. The player may
change the seven freely. It previews the relationships/events carried by each selection and never
labels an unselected realm lesser, invalid or erased.

## The completion act

The emotional verb is **joining**. The player is not restoring a single canonical Reality or
choosing which world is the original. They bind several lived, different realms so each remains
itself while routes between them become stable.

Completion is a deliberate sequence, not a boss fight and not a resource payment after the player
already met the requirements:

1. choose the folio;
2. review what will remain distinct and what connection will be created;
3. confirm the final binding;
4. perform one final authored world/route composition using vocabulary already earned; and
5. witness the joined Atlas, then continue playing in the same save.

The final composition should ask the player to express connection among their selected realms, not
solve one developer-authored “correct” pressure combination. Exact grammar waits until cross-realm
route representation exists; ordinary world-writing rules remain authoritative.

## Permanent payoff

Completing the volume unlocks **Atlas routes**: from a portal in one selected anchored realm, the
player may choose another selected realm as the destination instead of returning through Home.
Travel still commits a world turn at the origin/destination boundary and cannot bypass combat,
collapse settlement, dormancy or expedition-outcome banking. Exact economy should be designed with
the route transaction; the reward is a new way to inhabit the restored collection, not a percentage
multiplier.

Opening another volume later offers another player-chosen folio and route network. Existing routes
and realms remain. No volume is called a reset, difficulty tier or superior ending.

## Tam's missing act

Tam was an ordinary **rigger**: someone who aligned loads, held tension and knew when a line that
looked secure was actually pulling against itself. The Sundering changed their relation to seams;
Tam can now stand across the two sides of a live Atlas join long enough for another person to bind
it. They do not rebuild the Atlas for the player, author worlds, or become a secret chosen one.

Tam's unique endgame act is **Hold the join**:

- required once during the final binding of each new volume;
- possible only after the player has chosen an eligible folio and authored the connection;
- carries responsibility and discomfort, but no sacrifice/death or permanent party removal;
- adds no general combat technique, private meter, shop or repeatable field power; and
- cannot un-anchor or alter an existing realm.

Their ordinary work supplies the knowledge of tension, sequence and safe release; the Sundering
supplies the impossible ability to occupy both sides of a seam. This fulfills Tam's premise without
making them secretly exceptional before the disaster.

## Glass disposition

Tam does **not** receive Glass. Quartz remains precision material, Rift-glass remains unstable-boundary
material, and Glass may remain an independent fused-ground writing focus. If the eventual Atlas-route
transaction needs a physical stabilizer, Rift-glass is the semantically relevant existing material,
but the final binding proposed here should not become another late resource toll by default.

## Rejected shapes

- **Global destructive reset:** contradicts finality and removes accumulation rather than opening
  genuinely new play.
- **Every anchored realm increments a quota:** rewards cheap disposable worlds and turns the Atlas
  into a counter.
- **Hard diversity checklist:** makes authored similarity invalid and turns proportional world
  identity into one-of-each collection homework.
- **Final boss as completion gate:** shifts the campaign climax away from world authorship and
  restoration; optional opposition may occur earlier without owning the final verb.
- **Tam as material recipe or permanent sacrifice:** makes a person into a key item and undermines
  the restoration arc.

## Review questions

1. Does **joining** feel like the right final emotional act, with play continuing afterward?
2. Retire destructive Reality reset in favour of optional additional Atlas volumes?
3. Is the eligibility rule—anchor, revisit, one persistent relationship event—the right meaning of
   a realm the player has genuinely restored?
4. Use seven as the first comparison size, without a hard diversity checklist?
5. Is Tam's rigger → **Hold the join** premise worth developing into their full identity?

## If approved

Design must next specify folio/route persistence, final-composition grammar, Tam's ≤10-condition
signature and diary/meeting packet, and exact continuation disclosure. Engineering should preserve
current realm-history receipts meanwhile but implement no quota, route or Tam placeholder.
