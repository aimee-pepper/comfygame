# Traveller Identities — Sabine and Oda

**Status:** Identity directions approved; detailed prose remains to draft and review  
**Decision reflected:** Sabine is the sole Menagerist/Beast-handler. Oda owns all magical weapon
craft.  
**Terminology note:** The setting calls magic-like practice **the Art** and **emanation**. “Magic
weapons” remains the clear design category; character-facing prose can use the setting’s words.

> **Signature authority:** Sabine's seven-condition and Oda's eight-condition profiles are in
> `traveller-signatures-seven-eight-current.md`; exact thresholds remain playtest tuning.

## Pair contrast

These two should not feel like adjacent system owners who happened to be designed together.

| | **Sabine** | **Oda** |
|---|---|---|
| Central concern | How to be close to another living thing without owning it | How to make dangerous force usable without pretending it has become harmless |
| Habit | Watches for consent, routine and discomfort | Tests containment, grounding, release and what a weapon does to its bearer |
| Craft | Relationships with animals | Housings that hold or direct emanation at any reach |
| Combat expression | Fight as a pair | Deliver Craft effects through magical weapons |
| Failure she fears | Mistaking obedience for trust | Mistaking containment for safety or innocence |

They do not need a major relationship with each other. Their thematic contrast can remain something
the player notices rather than something the diaries explain.

# Sabine — Menagerist and Beast-handler

## Identity

| Field | Proposal |
|---|---|
| **Name** | **Sabine** |
| **Pronouns** | she/her |
| **Calling** | a menagerist and beast-handler |
| **Cast type** | Trade-capable traveller |
| **Campaign phase** | Late |
| **Signature complexity** | 7 conditions |
| **Building** | The Menagerie |
| **Teaching** | **Coral** |
| **Starting lean** | Protection + Precision, with a smaller Evasion lean |
| **Required?** | No |

### One-line blurb

> Kept animals for people who thought keeping meant owning. Learned most of her trade from being
> corrected.

### Voice anchors

- **Habit:** Notices what a creature is looking at, avoiding or waiting for before she notices what
  it looks like. Repairs tack and enclosures even when none are in use.
- **Attitude:** Patient without being soft-spoken; matter-of-fact about teeth, fear, hunger and
  injury. She does not turn animals into innocent little people.
- **Tension:** She believes trust must be continually renewed, but the Menagerie necessarily
  contains and assigns animals. She knows care can become control while still believing that care
  is worth attempting.

### Voice boundaries

- She uses concrete behavioural observations: weight shifting, ears turning, food left untouched,
  a path repeatedly chosen.
- She does not use baby talk, mystical “animal wisdom,” or claim animals are morally purer than
  people.
- She rarely says an animal “loves” or “hates” something when “returns to,” “avoids,” “permits” or
  “asks for” is more truthful.
- Her humour is dry and usually comes from humans being less legible than the creatures they fear.

## Why the player seeks her

Sabine opens an entire companion category rather than a passive numerical improvement:

- The **Menagerie** houses animal companions.
- She teaches taming in the field and how wants differ by generated creature traits.
- Trait inspection becomes more precise through observation rather than an authored species list.
- Animal commands and shared gambits allow a companion and animal to fight as a pair.
- Animals can later be assigned to anchored worlds where appropriate.

Sabine is both Menagerist and Beast-handler because keeping and field partnership are one literacy:
learning what a creature will tolerate, repeat and choose.

### Coherence sentence

> Because Sabine was a **menagerist who handled animals rather than displaying them**, she reads
> behaviour as negotiation, which is why recruiting her lets the player **tame, keep, understand
> and fight beside animal companions**.

## Coral

**Coral** remains a strong teaching if its world effect is living, collective structure rather than
simply “aquatic life.” It fits Sabine because coral challenges the boundary between creature, colony,
place and habitat—the same boundary the Menagerie asks the player to reconsider.

Her rune page should not say “coral is an animal” as trivia. It should describe a living structure
whose individual parts respond together, letting the player infer why this word belongs to her.

## Signature concept

Sabine is found in a world capable of sustaining varied animal life, but the signature should not
mean “maximum Vitality plus six arbitrary facts.” It should describe a **meeting ground**:

- abundant but not uniform life;
- reliable accessible water;
- at least two viable habitat bands or terrain transitions;
- moderate openness, allowing approach without total exposure;
- shelter or cover nearby;
- a stable enough cycle for routines to form;
- evidence of collective or colonial life, ideally Coral when vocabulary timing allows.

The final seven conditions must be writable with vocabulary available before her authored order and
must describe one believable place. Each receives its own location page.

## Diary shape — initial target: 10 pages

| Pages | Purpose |
|---:|---|
| 7 | One location page per signature condition |
| 1 | Coral teaching |
| 1 | Kestrel whereabouts or disagreement: studying an animal to kill it versus to live beside it |
| 1 | Menagerie/animal-companion knowledge carried through a site or remembered enclosure |

Character work belongs inside all ten pages. The location pages can reveal how she watches and what
she refuses to assume; they should not become neutral habitat reports.

### Diary arc

- **First impression:** Sabine is unusually certain that every creature can be read if watched long
  enough.
- **Complication:** Her pages reveal occasions when an animal complied because it had no alternative,
  and she mistook that compliance for trust.
- **After recruitment:** She has better practices, not perfect certainty. The Menagerie remains an
  attempt she expects the player to keep questioning.

## Meeting-scene direction

### Opening image

Sabine is repairing a braided lead she is not currently using. Several sets of tracks circle her camp;
none crosses the small boundary she has marked around herself. She notices the player stepping over
that line before she looks up.

### Three questions

1. **Where are the animals?** She describes proximity through signs rather than pointing to visible
   pets; they are near because they have chosen to remain near.
2. **Do they belong to you?** Her answer establishes the central distinction: responsibility is
   hers, ownership is not.
3. **How did you survive here?** She admits that animals showed her reliable routes and water, then
   corrects any romantic reading—they also stole food, drew predators and left when it suited them.

### Offer

> There’s a base. Help me make room for them.

### Acceptance direction

She does not immediately agree on the animals’ behalf. She agrees to inspect the place and carries
the unused lead rather than putting it on anything.

### Decline direction

She warns the player not to follow the clearest tracks; those belong to something deliberately
making itself easy to find.

## Relationships

- **Kestrel:** Respect and friction. Both observe creatures closely; their intended outcomes differ.
  Neither should be written as simply correct.
- **Sela:** Shares route knowledge and the habit of noticing where living things actually choose to
  go rather than where a map says they should.
- **Nessa:** Treatment, dosage and the danger of assuming a cure behaves identically across bodies.
- **Marrick or Bryn:** A useful disagreement over whether protection can be offered without taking
  control.

## Naming decision

**Sabine** replaces the earlier working name **Ilse**, which was too close visually and aurally to
Isolde for a diary-heavy game. Sabine is distinct across the current roster and fits the grounded,
slightly archaic naming register.

# Oda — Channelwright for magical weapons

## Identity

| Field | Proposal |
|---|---|
| **Name** | **Oda** *(working)* |
| **Pronouns** | she/her *(proposal)* |
| **Calling** | a **channelwright**—a maker of contained and projected emanation weapons |
| **Cast type** | Trade-capable traveller |
| **Campaign phase** | Late |
| **Signature complexity** | 7–8 conditions |
| **Building** | **The Channelworks** |
| **Teaching** | Exclusive **emanation housing schematic**; no world focus |
| **Starting lean** | Precision + Kindling, with Fortitude for safe containment |
| **Required?** | No |

### One-line blurb

> Gave dangerous force a handle, an edge, and somewhere to stop. Never calls that the same as making
> it safe.

### Voice anchors

- **Habit:** Tests housings until they fail, checks seams and grounding, and watches what prolonged
  use does to the bearer. At range she still marks safe lines and what lies beyond the target.
- **Attitude:** Exact, composed and wry. Treats spectacular effects as engineering problems and
  unimpressive containment as the greater accomplishment.
- **Tension:** A housing makes emanation usable and repeatable, which is not the same as making it
  safe or morally neutral. Oda continues making these weapons while distrusting the comfort their
  handles, guards and measured limits give the people holding them.

### Voice boundaries

- She says **carry, hold, channel, cast, arc, ground, discharge, reach**—not incantation, mana or
  destiny.
- She is not frightened of her own work and is not a volatile “mad inventor.” Caution is expertise,
  not cowardice.
- She values a weapon that stops exactly where intended more than one that makes an impressive
  crater.
- Her humour is understated: most warnings sound like observations made after somebody else already
  ignored them.

## Why the player seeks her

Oda opens a complete magical weapon category that neither the physical specialists nor Ashe supply:

- **The Channelworks** crafts magical weapons—setting-facing **contained and projected emanation
  implements**—at close, mid and far reach.
- These use reactive or emanant cores, conductive/focusing elements and insulating housings.
- Their identity is **Craft delivery and authored behaviour**, not superior raw damage.
- They let Venom, Shadow and the elemental Craft branch act through appropriate close or ranged
  weapon families and recipes.
- Higher tiers improve containment, reach where applicable, status reliability and shaped delivery
  rather than only increasing damage.

This preserves existing combat structure: magic weapons interact with the shared Craft tree
and existing damage/status systems rather than adding a separate spell list or mana progression.

### Current weapon-family direction

| Family | Delivery identity | Trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| **Contact housing** | Close release through a channelled edge, gauntlet or compact implement | Highest output; melee exposure |
| **Conduit housing** | Mid release across a controlled path | Balanced output and reach |
| **Projection housing** | Far release through lens and frame | Lowest output; far-reach safety |

All three may use heat, caustic or light attunement. The complete structural rules and implementation
order are in `channelworks-system-current.md`.

### Coherence sentence

> Because Oda was a **channelwright**, she knows how to give an emanation a usable shape without
> letting it consume its bearer, which is why recruiting her gives the player **magical weapons at
> every reach and their advanced recipes**.

## Teaching decision — housing schematic, not Arc

Oda's diary teaches an **emanation housing schematic** usable through the Channelworks after
recruitment. Arc is retired: it lacks a necessary world-writing purpose, suggests unsupported
electricity, and would exist mainly to fill the ledger.

## Signature concept

Oda is found where projection is naturally visible and dangerous—a **conductive open storm world**:

- strong atmospheric activity;
- frequent brief illumination or discharge;
- open relief with long clear lines;
- conductive or reactive substrate;
- enough hard material for shelter;
- limited tall growth or other obstructions;
- a cycle regular enough that she can predict the discharges;
- for an eighth condition, a scarce late focus that makes the place specifically hers rather than a
  generic storm plain.

The prose should describe paths of force, intervals and what survives between strikes. Do not expose
technical values or turn every clue into “there is lightning.”

## Diary shape — initial target: 11–12 pages

| Pages | Purpose |
|---:|---|
| 7–8 | One location page per signature condition |
| 1 | Arc or other final teaching |
| 1 | Ashe whereabouts/contrast: a person carrying what Oda normally puts in a housing |
| 1 | Fen or the physical Weaponsmith: the boundary between a physical weapon and one that houses or projects an emanation |
| 1 | Channelworks recipe/site history or a failed containment lesson |

### Diary arc

- **First impression:** Oda appears to believe every dangerous force can be made manageable if
  measured and housed well enough.
- **Complication:** Once dangerous force had a handle and repeatable form, people treated usability
  as proof of safety. Ranged forms added moral distance, but close forms created the same false
  confidence in a different way.
- **After recruitment:** She still makes them. Her answer is not abstinence but better containment,
  clearer consequence and refusing to call a bounded danger harmless.

## Meeting-scene direction

### Opening image

A pale line crosses the clearing before the player sees Oda. The last of several stone markers
splits; everything behind it remains untouched. Oda lowers a tube or rod, looks past the broken
marker rather than at it, and says the player has entered from the one direction she had cleared.

### Three questions

1. **What was that?** She explains a carried emanation in practical terms, focusing on its housing
   and stopping point rather than spectacle.
2. **Why shoot from so far away?** Safety is the first answer; responsibility is the more difficult
   one.
3. **Did you make weapons before the Sundering?** Yes. She rejects the comforting fiction that they
   were merely tools, while also refusing to pretend tools have only one use.

### Offer

> I have a workshop. You can build somewhere with walls behind the targets.

### Acceptance direction

She asks what the walls are made from before agreeing, then gathers the intact test housings and
leaves the spectacular broken one behind.

### Decline direction

She tells the player exactly which line is safe to take away from her range. It sounds courteous
until it becomes clear how carefully every other line has been tested.

## Relationships

- **Ashe:** The essential connection. Oda builds housings for emanation; Ashe’s body is one. Oda may
  initially treat Ashe as a containment problem, and must learn why that is dehumanising.
- **Fen:** Physical trajectory versus projected effect. They share distance craft without sharing a
  shop.
- **Halloway / the Weaponsmith:** Makes housings and frames that must survive Oda’s cores. Respectful
  arguments about whether a weapon that cannot be repaired safely should exist.
- **Nessa:** Reactive materials, prepared charges, dosage and the difference between poison carried
  in matter and an emanation carried through it.

## Shop naming decision

**The Channelworks** is approved over “Magic Shop,” “Arcane Workshop,” or a weapon-family name:

- it describes the actual craft—making an effect travel through a safe channel;
- it supports bound blades, rods, staves and mechanical casters without privileging one;
- it sits comfortably beside Blacksmith, Armoury, Weaponsmith and Deep Works;
- it leaves room for Oda’s identity to be more specific than the sign over her door.

**Channelwright** is the approved identity direction and current calling. It can still be challenged
during final prose review if it proves awkward in dialogue, but it no longer carries placeholder
status.

# Approved decisions and remaining check

Approved:

1. Sabine’s identity centres consent, routine and care without sentimentalising animals.
2. Sabine replaces the earlier working name Ilse.
3. Oda’s identity centres containment, consequence and responsibility; distance is one application.
4. Oda is a channelwright and owns **The Channelworks**.
5. Magic weapons are contained or projected emanation implements using the existing Craft branches,
   not a separate mana/spell system. The Channelworks owns close, mid and far magical families.

**Superseded after the vocabulary-purpose audit:** Arc is retired. Oda teaches the exclusive
emanation housing schematic defined in `channelworks-system-current.md`; she receives no world focus.
