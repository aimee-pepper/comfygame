# Auber and the Distillery — Identity and System Proposal

**Status:** Identity and system direction approved; exact recipes and numbers remain later work  
**Problem solved:** Give advanced essence a real decision and make Auber a person rather than an
upgrade menu  
**Boundary:** The existing Refinery keeps the simple raw essence → spendable essence conversion

> **Signature authority:** Auber's current eight-condition profile is in
> `traveller-signatures-auber-ashe-current.md`; exact thresholds remain playtest tuning.

## Distillery purpose

The Distillery should not improve the Refinery’s exchange rate. A better rate is useful but creates
no new way to play, and it makes Auber a tax-efficiency character.

**Recommendation:** the Distillery changes the **form and behaviour** of essence. Spendable essence
remains one currency. Distilled outputs are crafted objects or modifications, never additional
wallet balances.

### Three operations

| Operation | Input | Output | Player decision |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Crystallise** | Essence + a mineral world resource | An essence crystal used in high-tier recipes, Channelworks and anchoring | Which resource properties should the crystal inherit? |
| **Attune** | An essence crystal + a chosen world resource | A crystal biased toward a pressure, emanation or Craft behaviour | What kind of effect should this finite component carry? |
| **Infuse** | Essence + an existing crafted object/material | One chosen property or authored behaviour strengthened, with a compensating limit or cost | Which part of this object’s identity is worth intensifying? |

These operations are a design direction, not implementation-ready recipes. They share one rule:
**distillation concentrates difference; it never creates value from nothing.** A stronger chosen
property should narrow flexibility elsewhere, require a difficult catalyst, increase reactivity, or
consume a finite crystal.

### What this connects

- **The Channelworks:** magical weapons need contained emanant cores. Auber prepares the core; Oda
  makes it usable and safe enough to wield.
- **Anchoring:** stable crystals are credible components of permanent work, but should not make
  Adamant and every other anchoring material redundant.
- **Advanced equipment:** infused materials can support high-tier recipe trade-offs without becoming
  universal +1 upgrades.
- **Writing:** pressure-attuned crystals can carry the character of worlds the player deliberately
  authored, making world provenance matter to late crafting.

### Important implementation constraint

Raw essence is currently aggregated into one resource, so it no longer remembers which world
produced it. Do not fake provenance after aggregation. Either:

1. use a chosen **world resource**, which already carries properties;
   or
2. later introduce essence samples that preserve provenance as inventory instances.

**Decision:** begin with world resources. This uses the existing property system and avoids turning
every raw essence unit into a unique inventory object.

### One-line blurb

> Separates a thing from what carried it. Never calls what remains nothing.

### Voice anchors

- **Habit:** Lets mixtures settle before touching them. Keeps and labels residue other people would
  discard, because the remainder proves what the desired fraction cost.
- **Attitude:** Patient, exact and suspicious of purity language. He speaks about concentration as a
  physical fact, never a moral improvement.
- **Tension:** Distillation makes powerful things portable and repeatable by stripping away their
  context. Auber values what separation enables while worrying that every clean result teaches
  people to forget the world, material and waste it came from.

### Voice boundaries

- He uses **fraction, carrier, residue, vapour, settle, concentrate, recover**.
- He does not describe pure things as good or contaminated things as corrupt.
- He is not intoxicated, eccentric for its own sake, or framed through alcohol jokes.
- His humour comes from insisting that discarded fractions are still present somewhere, usually in
  the room everyone else has stopped looking at.

## Why the player seeks him

Auber opens late-game transformations rather than another source of essence:

- creates essence crystals for advanced recipes;
- attunes crystals through chosen world-derived catalysts;
- infuses an existing object toward one selected property or Craft behaviour;
- supplies cores to Oda and components to anchoring without owning either system;
- turns hoarded exceptional materials into catalysts with new value.

### Coherence sentence

> Because Auber was a **distiller**, he understands that concentration preserves one difference by
> discarding or relocating others, which is why recruiting him gives the player **crystallisation,
> attunement and infusion rather than a better currency exchange rate**.

## Brine

**Brine** fits Auber better than Amber. It is a mixture that visibly holds something dissolved, and
distillation reveals both the water and what the water carried. Its world-writing purpose is already
distinct: salt water, materially and ecologically different from ordinary Water, Sea or Salt alone.

As Auber’s exclusive teaching:

- remove its ordinary research route;
- keep it unavailable through generic wild/merchant acquisition;
- let his page describe the residue left after water is taken away rather than defining brine as a
  textbook term.

## Signature concept

Auber is found in a world that visibly separates and recombines matter:

- abundant saline or mineral-bearing water;
- strong but regular thermal cycling;
- evaporation and condensation both plausible;
- rich reactive or dissolved substrate;
- limited ordinary life but specialised life where appropriate;
- atmospheric movement sufficient to carry vapour;
- a stable enough cycle for repeated collection;
- one late precise condition that distinguishes his world from a generic salt flat.

The eight pages should collectively describe **a world functioning as a still**. No individual clue
should need to say “distillation.”

### Diary arc

- **First impression:** Auber seems to believe every mixture can be made legible by patient
  separation.
- **Complication:** One result was technically pure and practically disastrous because everyone
  ignored the concentrated waste and lost context.
- **After recruitment:** He still separates things. His practice now insists that every output names
  its carrier, catalyst and remainder.

## Meeting-scene direction

### Opening image

Auber has arranged several shallow vessels beneath a naturally dripping surface. He moves the nearly
empty vessel rather than the full one, then marks where its last ring dried. The residue interests him
more than the water.

### Questions

1. **What are you collecting?** He answers by naming both fractions: what rises or runs off, and what
   remains.
2. **Can you make essence here?** Essence is already here; the question is what form the player is
   willing to pay to keep it in.
3. **Why save the residue?** Because throwing something away only changes who must encounter it next.

### Offer

> There’s a refinery at the base. Come build the work it can’t do.

### Acceptance direction

He agrees after asking where the refinery’s waste goes. If the answer is “nowhere,” he says that
means nobody has followed it yet.

### Decline direction

He gives the player the clear fraction and keeps the cloudy remainder, warning that clarity is not
the same thing as safety.

## Relationships

- **Nessa:** Shared extraction knowledge, different purposes. Nessa asks what a dose does to a body;
  Auber asks what was concentrated to make the dose possible.
- **Grimmond:** Deep material enters Auber’s vessels. Their relationship makes extraction’s residue
  and environmental cost visible without turning either into a lecture.
- **Oda:** Auber produces emanant cores; Oda contains them. Each can identify where the other’s safety
  claims end.
- **Tovin:** Crystals tempt anchoring design. Auber should resist the assumption that stability in a
  vessel automatically means stability in a world.

## Approved direction

1. Keep the Refinery’s basic conversion and make the Distillery about **crystallise, attune and
   infuse**.
2. Keep spendable essence as one currency; distilled outputs are items/modifications.
3. Use existing **world resources** in Distillery operations before considering provenance-bearing
   raw essence instances.
4. Auber’s identity centres separation, residue and the danger of mistaking purity for moral
   improvement.
5. **Brine** replaces Amber and becomes diary-exclusive.
6. Treat exact recipes, costs and property changes as a later system-design pass.
