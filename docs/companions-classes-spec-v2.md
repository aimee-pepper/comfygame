<summary><b>1. What a companion is now</b></summary>

| Part | Source |
|---|---|
| **Name and story** | Authored (named travellers) or generated (generic) |
| **Class** | What they were before the sundering |
| **Stats** | Might · Finesse · Fortitude · Perception · Focus (session 17) |
| **Level and XP** | Grows from combat *and* discovery |
| **Skills** | Some from class, some learned |
| **Gear** | Eight slots, their own (session 17 / Q33) |
| **Gambits** | Their own rule list; slots governed by Focus |
| **Posting** | Party · Home · World |

**The through-line: a companion is a person with a past, and that past is mechanically load-bearing.** Their trade decides what they unlock at base, how they fight, and what they're good at in the field.

<summary><b>2. Classes — [AIMEE], proposal below</b></summary>

**The rule I'd suggest: every class does three things** — leans a stat, brings a combat role, and unlocks something at base. If a class doesn't do all three it's a title, not a class.

| Class | Stat lean | In a fight | Unlocks / field skill |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Smith** | Might, Fortitude | Front rank; crush weapons | **Blacksmith** — reforging, salvage |
| **Tanner** | Fortitude | Front rank; durable | **Tannery** — satchel and storehouse capacity (Q37) |
| **Apothecary** | Focus | Status, cures, coatings | **Apothecary** — consumables, inks, coated weapons |
| **Hunter** | Finesse, Perception | Pierce weapons; strikes first | Better butchery yields; reads creatures faster |
| **Archivist** | Focus, Perception | Weak; back rank | **Library** upgrades; extracts more from pages; faster rune study |
| **Delver** | Might, Fortitude | Front rank; heavy | Better mineral yields; can work hard substrate |
| **Forager** | Perception | Back rank; support | Better organic yields; identifies flora on sight |
| **Warden** | Fortitude | Front rank; draws attacks, protects the back | Reduces companion risk in assigned worlds |
| **Cartographer** | Perception, Focus | Back rank; reveals | Wider vision; more of the map revealed on entry |
| **Wright** | Might, Focus | Middling | Anchoring and tether work; cheaper base construction |
| **Trader** | Perception, Focus | Back rank; support and consumables | **The Exchange** — selling, buying, later the Recycler. Field skill: appraises grade on sight |

Eleven, and they cover the front/back spread that ranks need, all five stats, and every crafting building the design has named. **The Binder is separate and unique** — the player, not a class.

**Worth noting:** *Warden* is the answer to session 17's open question about the sustain economy. Companion risk in unstable worlds was removed, taking the teeth out of holding a greedy world. A Warden reducing that risk means the cost can come back as *needing the right person there* rather than as loss.

</details>

<summary><b>3. Recruitment — finding is not joining</b></summary>

**Finding someone puts them on the map. Recruiting them takes something.**

### Wants — who pays, and who doesn't (Aimee, 6 Aug)

**The split is by how you came to them.**

| | How you find them | Cost to recruit |
|---|---|---|
| **Named / diary travellers** | You **hunted** them — deduced a signature from pages and wrote your way there | **Nothing.** The hunt was the price, paid in literacy. |
| **Random companions** | You **stumbled** on them in a world written for other reasons | **A want** |
| **Animal companions** | Stumbled on | **A want**, plus taming |

**The people you worked to find cost nothing more; the people you got for free cost something.** (This inverts my earlier draft, which had it backwards.)

### Wants, for those who have them

| Want | Fits |
|---|---|
| **A material** with specific properties | Smith, Tanner, Apothecary |
| **A person found** first | Anyone with a connection in the diaries |
| **A place reached** — proof you can write your way somewhere | Cartographer, Wright |
| **Knowledge shown** — a rune, a completed diary | Archivist |
| **A creature brought or shown** | Hunter, Forager |
| **Nothing** — they just want out | The easiest random finds |

### The tavern

Met-but-unrecruited people turn up at the tavern and can be **asked** (clues) or **satisfied** there if their want is a material or knowledge. This is the tavern's real job: meeting someone stays valuable even when you couldn't recruit them on the spot.

</details>

<summary><b>4. Levelling</b></summary>

**XP from combat and discovery both** (session 17). **[PROPOSAL]** sources and rough weights:

| Source | Notes |
|---|---|
| Winning an encounter | Scaled by foe tier |
| **First sighting of a species** | The bestiary pays XP |
| Entering a site for the first time | |
| Recovering a page | |
| Reaching a named place | Large |
| Completing a diary | Large |

**Everyone in the party gains**, whether they acted or not — benching someone shouldn't leave them behind, because the search loop already forces composition changes.

**[PROPOSAL] Home and world postings gain reduced XP**, so an assigned companion doesn't fall permanently behind, but the party is still where growth happens.

### What a level gives
- **+stats**, weighted by class
- **Occasional skill unlock**, from a class list
- **Focus growth → more gambit slots**, so automation deepens as a person does

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>5. Postings</b></summary>

| Posting | Effect |
|---|---|
| **Party** | Fights, gains full XP, uses gear and gambits |
| **Home** | Works a station: faster research, better refining, Library throughput. Class decides which |
| **World** | Harvests an anchored world over runs; gambits govern their work |

**Reassignment is free and instant at base.** The decision is *who is best where*, not logistics.

**[PROPOSAL] A companion posted Home or World keeps their gear**, so moving someone isn't an equipment shuffle.

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>6. Animal companions</b></summary>

Tamed rather than recruited; **traits carry over from the world they came from**, so an animal from a strange world is genuinely unique.

- **No class, no wants.** They have traits, and traits do the work.
- **Can be posted** to party or world; the Menagerie holds the rest.
- **Can never die** (session 17) — they pass out like anyone.
- **[PROPOSAL]** taming odds depend on the creature's armament and conspicuousness — a grazer is easier than an apex — and on what you bring.

</details>

<summary><b>7. Build order</b></summary>

1. **Stats, levels, XP** on `CompanionState` (session 17) — everything else needs it.
2. **Classes as data**, with stat leans and starting skills.
3. **Recruitment**: wants, satisfying them, joining.
4. **Class → building unlock**, extending the existing `builtBy` mechanism. **Per Q40, each building then owns its own research tree** — so a person brings not just a building but everything that can be learned in it.
5. **Postings** and their effects.
6. **The tavern.**
7. **Animal companions and the Menagerie.**

</details>

<details>
<summary><b>8. What I'd want challenged</b></summary>

1. **Ten classes** — too many to author well, or too few to make a party feel chosen?
2. **Class as pre-sundering trade** — evocative, but it means every companion is a tradesperson. Where are the people who were nobody in particular?
3. **XP from discovery** — right for this game, but it means a careful player out-levels a fighter, and combat XP may end up vestigial.
4. **Warden reducing world risk** — it rescues the sustain economy, but it makes one class near-mandatory for anchoring.
5. **Whether generic travellers should have classes at all**, or stay simple bodies so the named cast stands out.
</details>
