# Traveller Identity Drafts — Bryn and Wren

**Status:** Identity, gambit and diary directions approved  
**Scope:** The remaining two dedicated fighters

> **Signature authority:** Bryn's current three-condition profile is in
> `traveller-signatures-early-mid-current.md`. Wren's five-condition profile is in

Bryn and Wren both prevent an enemy from carrying out its simplest plan, but through opposite
methods. Bryn makes herself the consequence. Wren makes the situation change before the consequence
can settle. Neither requires a new combat system: their identities clarify existing Protection,
Evasion, rank, enemy-count and tempo rules.

## Fighter contrast

| | **Bryn** | **Wren** |
|---|---|---|
| Combat answer | A vulnerable back rank | Several simultaneous foes |

| Central question | When does protection become control or debt? | When does adaptability become refusal to commit? |
| Existing skill anchor | Draw Off | Quicken |

# Bryn — Warden

## Identity

| Field | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Pronouns** | she/her |
| **Calling** | a warden who intervenes personally rather than commanding a formation |
| **Campaign phase** | Early–mid |
| **Signature complexity** | 3 conditions |
| **Teaching** | Exclusive gambit subject: **Ally: rank = back** |
| **Starting lean** | Protection + Fortitude, with Draw Off |

### One-line blurb

> Steps between danger and the person it chose. Knows being grateful is not the same as having agreed
> to owe her.

### Voice anchors

- **Habit:** Positions herself where she can see both the threat and the person behind her. After
  intervening, she asks what the protected person was trying to do rather than assuming safety was
  their only priority.
- **Attitude:** Plain, attentive and difficult to impress with endurance. Bryn treats taking a blow as
  a tactical exchange, not evidence of moral worth.
- **Tension:** Immediate protection cannot always wait for consent. But repeated intervention can
  quietly remove another person's agency, make gratitude compulsory, or teach Bryn to need being
  needed.

Bryn should not be a martyr whose only flaw is caring too much. She is genuinely skilled at reading
imminent harm and redirecting it. Her harder question is what responsibility remains after the danger
passes, when the person she protected may resent the choice or refuse the resulting debt.

## Why the player seeks her

- an early demonstration that party rank changes combat decisions;
- Draw Off and a strong Protection lean for guarding fragile companions;
- **Ally: rank = back**, enabling gambits that protect by tactical position rather than naming one
  permanent vulnerable person;
- personal interception and damage-sharing tools distinct from Marrick's later party-wide routines.

Marrick designs coordination before danger selects anyone. Bryn intervenes after the danger becomes
particular. A formation can reduce how often rescue is needed; it cannot replace the judgement or
relationship involved when Bryn chooses to take one person's consequence.

### Coherence sentence

> Because Bryn learned to **recognise when danger has selected someone with little room to answer**,
> she teaches the party to respond to an **ally in the back rank** and redirect immediate harm.

## Signature and diary shape

Her three-condition world should repeatedly place something vulnerable behind something exposed: a
hazard arriving from one legible direction, fragile life or resources that cannot simply relocate,
and terrain where interception is possible but costly. The signature must permit several protective
responses rather than proving that Bryn's body is the only valid barrier.

**Initial target: 7 pages**

| Pages | Purpose |
|---:|---|
| 3 | One location page per signature condition |
| 1 | Back-rank ally gambit teaching |
| 1 | A protection that was necessary but unwanted |
| 1 | Marrick, Bracken or Ashe relationship page |
| 1 | Breached shelter, diverted threat or Bryn whereabouts page |

### Diary arc

- **First impression:** Bryn appears to know exactly when another person needs her intervention.
- **Complication:** A person she repeatedly protected became safer and less able to act, while Bryn
  interpreted their dependence as proof that she should continue.
- **After recruitment:** She still acts before permission when harm is immediate. She becomes more
  disciplined about returning choice afterward and refusing to turn rescue into authority.

## Meeting-scene direction

Bryn is holding a route open from the dangerous side, but the people or creatures behind her are not
using it. She has correctly stopped the threat and incorrectly assumed where everyone wanted to go.
The player's conversation begins after the impressive part, when she must decide whether to keep
holding, move, or listen.

## Relationship anchors

- **Marrick:** Personal intervention versus protective infrastructure. Each solves failures the other
  cannot, and each can create a different form of dependency.
- **Bracken:** Armour moves cost into the wearer; Bryn moves danger into herself. Neither transfer
  makes the cost disappear.
- **Ashe:** Other people have repeatedly mistaken capacity to endure for consent to be used.
- **Talin:** Both sometimes act before consent or certainty is available and must own what follows.

# Wren — Skirmisher

## Identity

| Field | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Pronouns** | he/him |
| **Calling** | a skirmisher who manages crowded threats through tempo and exits |
| **Campaign phase** | Mid–late |
| **Signature complexity** | 5 conditions |
| **Teaching** | Exclusive gambit threshold: **Foes present ≥ 3** |
| **Starting lean** | Evasion + Swiftness, with Quicken |

### One-line blurb

> Finds the exit before he needs it. Calls speed a debt owed to the moment after.

### Voice anchors

- **Habit:** Counts moving parts, empty spaces and exits. In a crowd he watches where pressure will be
  after the next movement, not where everybody currently stands.
- **Attitude:** Adaptive, wry and unusually honest about fatigue. Wren dislikes stories in which acting
  twice somehow means time was created rather than borrowed.
- **Tension:** Keeping several options open lets him survive rapidly changing situations. It can also
  become a way to avoid being present when other people need a choice to remain chosen.

Wren is not “the quick one.” His authored concern is **tempo debt**: every burst constrains the next
moment. That gives Quicken's existing “act twice next turn, then skip one” rule a human and tactical
meaning without adding a new meter.

## Why the player seeks him

- Evasion and multi-target tools for encounters that become dense or unpredictable;
- Quicken as an explicit burst-and-recovery choice;
- **Foes present ≥ 3**, enabling the player to reserve crowd tools for actual crowds;
- a fighter who changes action order and threat distribution rather than simply attacking faster.

Sela moves because she wants to know what is beyond the current route; movement expands her world.
Wren moves because too many things are happening for the current arrangement to remain safe;
movement preserves options. Their energy can look similar while their reasons are nearly opposite.

### Coherence sentence

> Because Wren learned to **read pressure across several moving threats and account for the recovery
> after a burst**, he teaches the party to react when **three or more foes are present** and use tempo
> without pretending it is free.

## Signature and diary shape

His five-condition world should be crowded without being merely hostile: several creature routes,
moving environmental pressure, broken sightlines, multiple exits and a cycle condition that changes
which opening is safest. It should reward re-evaluation, not constant movement for its own sake.

**Initial target: 9 pages**

| Pages | Purpose |
|---:|---|
| 5 | One pressure, route or exit page per signature condition |
| 1 | Foe-count gambit teaching |
| 1 | Sela relationship page: curiosity-driven movement versus tactical repositioning |
| 1 | A commitment Wren escaped so successfully that somebody else had to finish it |
| 1 | Convergence site, blocked exit or Wren whereabouts page |

### Diary arc

- **First impression:** Wren appears impossible to corner and able to improvise around any change.
- **Complication:** He has sometimes treated leaving a bad arrangement as the same thing as resolving
  it, passing the constrained aftermath to people with fewer exits.
- **After recruitment:** He remains adaptable but learns to distinguish an exit from an abandonment
  and to budget recovery where others can rely on it.

## Meeting-scene direction

Wren has escaped a convergence of several threats and is calmly predicting how they will interfere
with one another. The problem is that the interference will soon close the only route available to
someone slower. Recruiting him begins with whether he uses the opening to leave or spends it changing
the pattern for somebody else.

## Relationship anchors

- **Sela:** Similar visible energy, opposite purpose. Curiosity expands possibilities; tactical
  movement preserves them under pressure.
- **Dagg:** Wren borrows from his next moment while Dagg protects the quality of the next commitment.
- **Rook:** Rook makes one approach legible; Wren reads what happens when several approaches collapse
  together.
- **Marrick:** Improvised adaptation versus routines stable enough to survive fear and fatigue.

# Complexity check

Neither identity adds a player-facing subsystem.

- Bryn uses existing ranks, Draw Off, Protection and an ally-position gambit.
- Wren uses existing enemy count, Quicken, Evasion and Swiftness.
- “Tempo debt” is explanatory language for Quicken's existing skip turn, not a new resource.
- Their signatures use ordinary world conditions and one clue per condition.

# Approved batch decisions

1. **Bryn:** protection-without-ownership is her central identity.
2. **Wren:** tempo debt and preserving options are his central identity.
3. Keep their existing exclusive gambits unchanged: **Ally: rank = back** and **Foes present ≥ 3**.
4. Diary targets: Bryn 7 pages and Wren 9 pages.
5. Relationship contrasts: Marrick/Ashe for Bryn and Sela/Dagg for Wren.
