# Traveller Identity Drafts — Bracken, Fen and Maud

**Status:** Identity directions approved; detailed prose remains to draft and review  
**Scope:** The three advanced physical-equipment specialists  
**Settled shop structure:** Armoury = armour · Bowyer = non-magical ranged · Weaponsmith =
non-magical melee

> **Signature authority:** Bracken, Fen and Maud's current five-condition profiles are in
> `traveller-signatures-five-condition-current.md`; exact thresholds remain playtest tuning.

Halloway remains the foundation beneath all three. Her Blacksmith handles ordinary equipment,
repair, reforging and salvage. These specialists do not make her obsolete; they teach narrower work
that becomes worthwhile after the player understands ordinary gear.

## Craft contrast

| | **Bracken** | **Fen** | **Maud** |
|---|---|---|---|
| Makes | Advanced armour | Physical ranged weapons | Advanced physical melee weapons |
| Central concern | What protection reveals about the harm it survived | What must happen before release so the shot needs no correction | How a weapon fits the person willing to close with it |
| Material literacy | Layers, resistance, load, failure | Tension, flex, balance, flight | Edge, weight, leverage, grip |
| Failure feared | Armour that saves the body by making it unusable | A beautiful shot built on an assumption nobody checked | A perfect weapon made for an imaginary wielder |
| Relationship to distance | Absorbs what reached you | Solves the path before contact | Accepts that contact is the point |

None should speak as though their shop owns an objective “best” item. Their advanced recipes should
create sharper trade-offs and better fit, not a simple replacement ladder.

# Bracken — Armorer

## Identity

| Field | Proposal |
|---|---|
| **Name** | **Bracken** *(working)* |
| **Pronouns** | he/him *(proposal)* |
| **Calling** | an armorer |
| **Cast type** | Trade-capable traveller |
| **Campaign phase** | Mid |
| **Signature complexity** | 5 conditions |
| **Building** | The Armoury |
| **Teaching** | **Bone** |
| **Starting lean** | Fortitude + Protection, with a smaller Force lean |
| **Required?** | No |

### One-line blurb

> Reads a dent from the inside. Says armour records the blow more honestly than the person who took
> it.

### Voice anchors

- **Habit:** Turns damaged objects over and studies their hidden surfaces. Checks straps, joints and
  range of motion before asking whether a plate stopped the blow.
- **Attitude:** Careful, unsentimental and quietly attentive. He asks where something hurts without
  treating pain as weakness or proof of character.
- **Tension:** Armour can prevent injury by transferring cost into heat, weight, immobility and
  exhaustion. Bracken distrusts protection that lets someone survive while making it impossible for
  them to keep living or choosing.

### Voice boundaries

- He describes consequences: buckling, bruising, pinching, trapped heat, shortened breath.
- He does not glorify “taking a hit,” divide people into strong and fragile, or promise invulnerability.
- His humour is literal and mild. If someone calls armour impenetrable, he asks from which direction.
- He notices when another person is concealing discomfort but does not announce it publicly.

## Why the player seeks him

The Armoury adds armour decisions unavailable at the ordinary Blacksmith:

- higher-tier armour recipes and upgrade paths;
- resistance-oriented construction using insulation, reactivity and specialised layers;
- deliberate defence/mobility trade-offs rather than tier-only stat increases;
- fitting and refitting so advanced armour can follow a companion’s build;
- later recipes that redirect, disperse or convert harm at a cost elsewhere.

Bracken should not simply sell armour with larger numbers. A good Armoury choice answers **what harm
this person expects and what cost they can afford to carry**.

### Coherence sentence

> Because Bracken was an **armorer who studied what happened behind the plate**, he understands the
> costs protection moves rather than erases, which is why recruiting him gives the player **advanced
> armour recipes, resistance construction and meaningful defensive trade-offs**.

## Bone

**Bone** fits because it is protection, support and evidence of stress at once. It is not simply a
hard crafting material. Bracken’s page should notice how bone remodels around repeated load or how a
break reveals the direction of force.

The world-writing purpose must remain distinct from Chitin and high Vitality: Bone means that death,
predation or accumulated remains occurred at scale, not merely that living things have skeletons.

## Signature concept

Bracken is found in a world where exposure is survivable only through layered protection:

- strong environmental or creature pressure;
- large thermal range or another condition that defeats one-note armour;
- abundant hard material;
- enough living material for flexible joints and bindings;
- enclosed or broken relief where mobility still matters.

The five clues should describe **a place that punishes rigid solutions**. Avoid making his signature
“hard world for the armour man”; the combination should explain why he learned to distrust armour
that solves only one problem.

## Diary shape — initial target: 8 pages

| Pages | Purpose |
|---:|---|
| 5 | One location page per condition |
| 1 | Bone teaching |
| 1 | Corrin or Halloway relationship page: flexible layers versus rigid protection |
| 1 | Failed armour/site or Bryn whereabouts page |

### Diary arc

- **First impression:** Bracken appears able to infer every blow from what it left behind.
- **Complication:** One failure came from a suit working exactly as designed while preventing its
  wearer from escaping, breathing freely or admitting distress.
- **After recruitment:** He still believes in protection. He no longer permits “survived” to be the
  only measure of success.

## Meeting-scene direction

### Opening image

Bracken is cutting apart a piece of armour that appears intact. The outer surface is barely marked;
the padding beneath it has been crushed flat. He asks the player to hold one edge, then corrects
where they grip so the hidden buckle does not cut them.

### Questions

1. **Why ruin armour that still works?** It does not still work; the outside simply retained its
   shape.
2. **Who wore it?** He answers through what the damage proves and refuses to invent the rest.
3. **Can you make something nothing gets through?** Yes, briefly, if the wearer agrees not to move,
   breathe well or leave.

### Offer

> There’s a forge at the base. We need somewhere that can make what comes after it.

### Acceptance direction

He asks whether Halloway is there. Her presence makes him more willing, not less; advanced work
without repair and salvage would be vanity.

### Decline direction

He gives the player the intact-looking outer plate and keeps the ruined lining, because the lining is
the part with something left to teach.

## Relationships

- **Halloway:** Mutual respect. She keeps equipment in use; he specialises after ordinary work stops
  being enough. He worries specialisation can become preciousness.
- **Corrin:** The most important material disagreement. Soft layers and flexible fittings often save
  the body more effectively than impressive plate.
- **Maud:** Armour and melee weapon design shape one another. Their arguments should be about bodies
  and movement, not “offence versus defence.”
- **Bryn:** Bracken questions protection that requires one person to absorb every consequence for
  everyone else.

# Fen — Bowyer and physical-ranged specialist

## Identity

| Field | Proposal |
|---|---|
| **Name** | **Fen** *(working)* |
| **Pronouns** | they/them *(proposal)* |
| **Calling** | a bowyer and maker of physical ranged weapons |
| **Cast type** | Trade-capable traveller |
| **Campaign phase** | Mid |
| **Signature complexity** | 5 conditions |
| **Building** | The Bowyer |
| **Teaching** | **Silk** |
| **Starting lean** | Precision + Swiftness, with a smaller Evasion lean |
| **Required?** | No |

### One-line blurb

> Makes the shot long before anything is released. The rest is string remembering what it was told.

### Voice anchors

- **Habit:** Tests flex, balance and repeated motion. Plucks cordage to hear uneven tension and watches
  wind disturb small hanging fibres.
- **Attitude:** Preparatory, economical and comfortable waiting. Fen does not romanticise instinct;
  a clean release is usually the result of many quiet corrections made earlier.
- **Tension:** A ranged weapon rewards prediction, but prediction can harden into deciding what a
  distant person or creature will do before they have done it.

### Voice boundaries

- Fen talks about paths, lead, tension, recovery and what changes between release and arrival.
- They are not aloof merely because their craft works at distance.
- They dislike “perfect shot” stories that erase material preparation, weather and luck.
- Their humour is sparse and timed; they are willing to let silence do part of the work.

## Why the player seeks them

The Bowyer owns every advanced **non-magical ranged** family:

- bows and related tension weapons;
- mechanical launchers such as crossbow-like families;
- slings or other flexible launchers if they create a real tactical identity;
- thrown weapon families where recovery, weight and trajectory matter;
- ammunition or prepared-projectile choices only if they add decisions without inventory chores.

The shop’s common language is physical trajectory, not a historical definition of “bow.” Its
advanced upgrades improve range, opening actions, target selection, recovery and shaped physical
delivery. It does not craft magical projectiles; those belong entirely to Oda’s Channelworks.

### Coherence sentence

> Because Fen was a **bowyer who understood the whole path between hand and target**, they know how
> flex, balance and release become range, which is why recruiting them gives the player **advanced
> non-magical ranged weapons and their tactical upgrades**.

## Silk

**Silk** fits through tension, flexibility, fine cordage and living material. Fen should not teach it
as “better bowstring.” Their page should reveal the more interesting literacy: many weak continuous
threads sharing load can outperform one apparently strong line.

Its world meaning—something spins here—remains independently useful for writing life and locating
silk-producing ecologies.

## Signature concept

Fen is found in a world where long physical paths can be observed and predicted:

- open relief or long sightlines;
- persistent but legible wind rather than still air;
- fibrous or silk-producing life;
- moderate vertical variation that makes trajectory visible;
- a reliable cycle or repeated atmospheric pattern.

This is not simply the ideal place to shoot. It is a world where **nothing travels straight without
being corrected for something**, which better reflects Fen’s identity.

## Diary shape — initial target: 8 pages

| Pages | Purpose |
|---:|---|
| 5 | One location page per condition |
| 1 | Silk teaching |
| 1 | Rook or Kestrel relationship/whereabouts page |
| 1 | Ranged-weapon site, failed shot or Oda contrast page |

### Diary arc

- **First impression:** Fen appears to believe preparation can remove uncertainty.
- **Complication:** A carefully predicted action failed because the target chose something Fen had
  never allowed for.
- **After recruitment:** Fen continues preparing meticulously, but leaves deliberate room for another
  living thing to surprise them.

## Meeting-scene direction

### Opening image

Fen has strings of different materials tied between two stones. None is attached to a weapon. They
pluck one, wait for the wind to answer through it, and cut it down without firing anything.

### Questions

1. **Are you making a bow?** No; they are finding out which material will still be the same after a
   hundred draws.
2. **What are you aiming at?** Nothing yet. Choosing a target before understanding the path is how
   people blame the wind for their own work.
3. **Did you hunt?** Sometimes. Making a weapon and deciding where to point it were different jobs,
   even when the same person did both.

### Offer

> Come back with me. There are materials there worth testing.

### Acceptance direction

Fen takes only the cord that behaved consistently, not the one that held the greatest single load.

### Decline direction

They tell the player when the crosswind will turn and which exposed route to avoid until then.

## Relationships

- **Rook:** Reach as field discipline versus reach as constructed trajectory. They should improve
  one another’s techniques without collapsing into the same role.
- **Kestrel:** Fen makes tools; Kestrel chooses living targets. Their disagreement is about how much
  responsibility travels backward from use into making.
- **Oda:** Physical trajectory versus contained/projected emanation. Shared vocabulary around range,
  very different failure modes.
- **Maud:** Fen solves travel before contact; Maud solves leverage after contact. Material arguments
  can reveal both.

# Maud — Weaponsmith

## Identity

| Field | Proposal |
|---|---|
| **Name** | **Maud** |
| **Pronouns** | she/her *(proposal)* |
| **Calling** | a weaponsmith |
| **Cast type** | Trade-capable traveller |
| **Campaign phase** | Mid–late |
| **Signature complexity** | 5–6 conditions |
| **Building** | The Weaponsmith |
| **Teaching** | Exclusive **fitting pattern**; no world focus |
| **Starting lean** | Force + Precision, with a smaller Fortitude lean |
| **Required?** | No |

### One-line blurb

> Fits a weapon to the hand, the shoulder, and how close its bearer is willing to stand. Distrusts
> anything praised without naming who must use it.

### Voice anchors

- **Habit:** Hands an unfinished object to someone before deciding its final balance. Watches grip,
  stance and compensation rather than asking whether it feels impressive.
- **Attitude:** Direct, collaborative and difficult to flatter. Maud treats advanced craft as closer
  attention to a particular person, not secret mastery above ordinary smithing.
- **Tension:** A fitted weapon respects its bearer, but it also makes harming someone easier and more
  personal. Maud refuses both comforting extremes: that makers bear no responsibility, or that a
  weapon has only the use its maker imagined.

### Voice boundaries

- She talks about leverage, recovery, edge alignment, grip and fatigue.
- She never calls a weapon objectively best without specifying the hand, body, opponent and intended
  distance.
- She is not a mystical master-smith, perfectionist recluse or superior replacement for Halloway.
- Her humour comes from puncturing heroic weapon language with bodily facts.

## Why the player seeks her

The Weaponsmith owns advanced **non-magical melee** construction:

- higher-tier blades, crushing weapons, close piercing weapons and melee reach families;
- upgrade paths that change balance, recovery, armour interaction and attack delivery;
- fitted weapons that interact with a companion’s build or preferred role;
- advanced recipes that intensify trade-offs rather than raising every statistic;
- no armour, physical ranged or magical weapons—those belong to Bracken, Fen and Oda.

The physical ranged/melee boundary should follow combat delivery. A spear used to hold a line or
strike from mid reach is Weaponsmith work; a purpose-built thrown spear belongs to Fen.

### Coherence sentence

> Because Maud was a **weaponsmith who fitted melee weapons to real bodies rather than ideal forms**,
> she understands leverage, recovery and chosen proximity, which is why recruiting her gives the
> player **advanced non-magical melee recipes and fitted upgrades**.

## Teaching decision

Do not assign Maud a focus merely because every trade row expects one. The best current candidates
all have complications:

- **Obsidian** fits edges but already has ordinary wild/merchant acquisition.
- **Adamant** fits endgame weapon work but is overloaded by anchoring.
- **Glass** collides with Tam’s unresolved premise and resource question.
- A new word such as **Edge** risks being a crafting label without a distinct world-writing purpose.

Maud teaches an exclusive **fitting pattern**: the first advanced melee recipe whose balance and
recovery are fitted to a real wielder. It is a recipe/research lead usable through the Weaponsmith,
not a new equipment slot or character-bound item system. Obsidian, Adamant, Glass and a new Edge rune
remain rejected for the reasons above.

## Signature concept

Maud must not repeat Halloway’s early hot-and-ductile signature. Her world should contain a more
specific **balance of opposed material demands**:

- workable material and hard material both present;
- enough heat to shape without making the whole world simply “the hot forge place”;
- broken or varied relief suitable for testing close reach and footing;
- a pressure that rewards recovery and control rather than raw force;
- for a sixth condition only if the finished five-condition place lacks sufficient specificity; do
  not add a material solely to mirror her non-focus teaching.

The place should read as somewhere no single “best material” solves every weapon.

## Diary shape — initial target: 8–9 pages

| Pages | Purpose |
|---:|---|
| 5–6 | One location page per condition |
| 1 | Fitting-pattern knowledge |
| 1 | Halloway relationship page |
| 1 | Bracken, Fen or Dagg whereabouts/material disagreement |

### Diary arc

- **First impression:** Maud appears to believe every weapon problem can be solved by fitting the
  object more precisely to its user.
- **Complication:** A perfectly fitted weapon made one person extraordinarily effective at something
  Maud had assumed they would never choose to do.
- **After recruitment:** She keeps fitting weapons. Her responsibility lies in refusing abstraction,
  not pretending she can control another person through the object she makes.

## Meeting-scene direction

### Opening image

Maud asks the player to hold a plain iron bar before she introduces herself. She turns their wrist,
moves one hand half a span, then takes the bar back and marks it where their grip changed—not where
its mathematical centre lies.

### Questions

1. **Is that a weapon?** Not yet; it has not been made for anyone or any use.
2. **What makes a weapon advanced?** Knowing exactly which compromise was chosen and for whom, not
  adding ornament or rare material.
3. **Did Halloway teach you?** Their exact history remains open, but the answer should establish
  respect without making Maud Halloway’s linear apprentice or superior.

### Offer

> Halloway keeps the forge. Come build the work that needs a hand beside hers.

### Acceptance direction

Maud corrects “beside” to something more precise—near enough to share heat, far enough not to share
an anvil—then agrees.

### Decline direction

She gives the player the marked iron bar and tells them to notice how quickly they return to the same
grip when frightened.

## Relationships

- **Halloway:** Essential. Halloway is foundation, repair and continued use; Maud is specialised fit.
  Neither is teacher, replacement or upgrade embodied as a person.
- **Bracken:** Movement, reach and protection are co-designed around the same body.
- **Fen:** Constructed distance versus chosen proximity.
- **Dagg or Talin:** A fighter tests whether Maud’s fitted theory survives someone with an already
  developed technique.

# Approved decisions

1. **Bracken** is a careful reader of transferred harm, not a toughness archetype.
2. **Fen** focuses on anticipation, material preparation and physical trajectory.
3. **Maud** is the Weaponsmith: collaborative fitting, leverage and chosen proximity.
4. The advanced shops are horizontal specialisations with sharper trade-offs, not automatic tier
   replacements.
5. **Bone** and **Silk** are exclusive diary teachings from Bracken and Fen; remove their ordinary
   wild/merchant acquisition routes.
6. Maud teaches an exclusive fitting pattern through the Weaponsmith; she has no world focus.
7. **Maud** is approved as the new Weaponsmith’s name. Proposed pronouns remain editable with later
   prose if needed.
