## Shared meeting shape

Each traveller meeting contains:

1. one concrete opening action that can be understood before any question;
2. up to three optional, independent question/reply pairs with stable semantic IDs;
3. one concise **player-spoken invitation** that names what joining changes without reading like a
   feature-list tooltip;
4. separate accepted and declined replies, each shown before dismissal;
5. no hidden correct answer, relationship score, exhaustive-question requirement or world-turn tax.

The three question roles are **practice**, **tension** and **presence**: what the traveller is doing,
what that practice gets wrong or costs, and why this particular world/moment matters. They are roles,
not mandatory wording. Every reply must stand alone because the player may ask in any order or not at
all. `traveller-meeting-interaction-current.md` owns transcript and terminal-state behavior.

Openings show the identity before explaining it. Do not begin with a thesis, profession label or
compressed biography. Meetings may imply a station, teaching or combat value, but recruitment does
not require the player to promise a building, agree with the traveller or solve their central
tension.

`offer` is the text on the player's recruitment button and must read in the player's voice. The
traveller does not invite themself home, describe “your party,” promise “I can build,” or ask the
player to make room for the traveller to try. `accepted` and `declined` are the traveller's terminal
responses. Atlas preview validates the transition aloud as **player offer → traveller response**.

## Acceptance for a complete corpus

- All 29 travellers have one schema-valid meeting object and globally stable exchange IDs.
- Openings, replies, offer and terminal copy render correctly on compact phone and VoiceOver.
- The three optional replies remain coherent in all six possible tap orders and when skipped.
- Every offer agrees with the current station/combat/teaching fiction but recruitment never requires
  a building promise or profession quiz.
- Reading every terminal transition as speaker-labelled text produces player offer → traveller
  accepted/declined response; no offer is accidentally authored in the traveller's voice.
- Atlas review state and exact text hash survive copy iteration outside campaign saves.
- A missing or stale review record is visible to developers and never silently treated as Good.
