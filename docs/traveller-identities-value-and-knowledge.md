# Traveller Identity Drafts — Vance, Kestrel, Lys and Ashe

**Status:** Identity directions approved; detailed prose and combat balance remain to draft  
**Scope:** Value, creature knowledge, archives and embodied emanation

> **Signature authority:** Vance's three-condition profile is in
> `traveller-signatures-early-mid-current.md`; Lys's four-condition profile is in
> `traveller-signatures-mid-current.md`; Kestrel's five-condition profile is in
> `traveller-signatures-five-condition-current.md`. Ashe's eight-condition profile is in
> `traveller-signatures-auber-ashe-current.md`.

These four travellers all interpret what the player finds, but they must not collapse into four
forms of “expert.” Vance asks what should circulate. Kestrel asks what observation permits. Lys asks
what a record cannot preserve. Ashe asks who gets to decide what a dangerous body is for.

## Identity contrast

| | **Vance** | **Kestrel** | **Lys** | **Ashe** |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Reads | Objects in circulation | Living creatures and their traces | Records in relation to other records | Emanation as bodily sensation |

# Vance — Trader

## Identity

| Field | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Pronouns** | he/him |
| **Calling** | a trader and appraiser |
| **Building** | The Exchange, including the Recycler |
| **Campaign phase** | Mid |
| **Signature complexity** | 3 conditions |
| **Teaching** | **Amber**, diary-exclusive |
| **Starting lean** | Precision + Ingenuity |

### One-line blurb

> Remembers who carried a thing before he remembers what it costs. Insists that this makes him a
> better trader, not a sentimental one.

### Voice anchors

- **Habit:** Examines wear, repairs and alterations before pristine surfaces. He often knows an
  object's likely route before its origin.
- **Attitude:** Sociable without being ingratiating; exact about prices and candid about uncertainty.
  He does not pretend a fair exchange must feel equal to both people.
- **Tension:** Circulation keeps useful things from becoming hoards, but pricing can flatten need,
  history and affection into one comparable number.

Vance should never be a generic charming merchant or a walking economy tutorial. His expertise is
provenance in motion: how objects acquire meaning by passing between people, and how markets erase
that history while depending on it.

## Why the player seeks him

The Exchange turns surplus into choice:

- buying and selling ordinary goods in useful quantities;
- bulk exchanges that reduce repetitive transactions;
- appraisal that exposes value, rarity or likely use;
- recycling unwanted items into recoverable world resources;
- later exchanges shaped by supply, relationships or unusual provenance.

The Recycler should recover usefulness, not reproduce the Distillery or Blacksmith. It breaks down
finished surplus; Auber transforms selected world resources with essence; Halloway repairs, reforges
and salvages equipment.

### Coherence sentence

> Because Vance followed **objects through many owners rather than judging them at first glance**, he
> understands both recoverable material and negotiated value, which is why recruiting him opens the
> **Exchange, appraisal and Recycler**.

## Amber

Amber is the deliberate contradiction in Vance's identity: a preserved thing taught by someone who
believes objects should move. His page should ask when preservation protects a history and when it
removes something from use forever.

This makes Amber stronger here than as a hunter's material. Amber is Vance's diary-exclusive focus;
its former late-wild/merchant route is superseded.

## Signature and diary shape

Vance's three-condition world should be a natural exchange route: two materially distinct regions
or ecologies connected by a traversable bottleneck, with evidence that objects moved between them.

**Initial target: 7 pages**

| Pages | Purpose |
|---:|---|
| 3 | One location page per signature condition |
| 1 | Amber teaching |
| 1 | A repaired object whose successive owners changed its use |
| 1 | Orsa, Corrin or Auber relationship page |
| 1 | Failed exchange, abandoned cache or Vance whereabouts page |

### Diary arc

- **First impression:** Vance can seemingly turn anything unwanted into something useful.
- **Complication:** He once kept a vital object circulating by concealing why its previous owner had
  refused to sell it.
- **After recruitment:** He remains a trader. What changes is his willingness to treat provenance as
  part of informed consent rather than private leverage.

## Relationship anchors

- **Orsa:** Trade as repeated relationship versus trade as a completed price.
- **Auber:** Vance sees potential supply where Auber sees a resource whose history may matter.
- **Halloway:** Both resist waste; they disagree over whether every damaged thing should re-enter use.
- **Lys:** An archive removes things from circulation but preserves relationships markets forget.

# Kestrel — Hunter

## Identity

| Field | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Pronouns** | she/her |
| **Calling** | a hunter and field naturalist |
| **Cast type** | Dedicated fighter |
| **Campaign phase** | Mid |
| **Signature complexity** | 5 conditions |
| **Teaching** | Exclusive gambit subject: **Foe: unrecorded species** |
| **Starting lean** | Precision + Force, with field utility |

### One-line blurb

> Learns what an animal does when nobody is pursuing it. Decides what to kill only after that.

### Voice anchors

- **Habit:** Reads interrupted behaviour: a feeding route abandoned, an uneven track, a call that
  stopped too early. She observes before naming.
- **Attitude:** Practical about predation and butchery. She neither sanitises killing nor turns it
  into a ceremony that makes the hunter feel innocent.
- **Tension:** Close study can prevent waste and needless violence, but expertise can become a claim
  of entitlement: believing that understanding a creature grants the right to manage or take it.

Kestrel is not “the ethical hunter” whose respect absolves every kill. Her actual ethic is narrower
and harder: do not make ignorance somebody else's injury, and do not call waste inevitable when it
was merely convenient.

## Why the player seeks her

- improved yields when butchering creatures whose anatomy the party has recorded;
- tracking information from traces, movement and habitats;
- bestiary knowledge that supports preparation rather than only documenting past kills;
- a combat teaching that lets the player automate discovery before violence.

### Recommended exclusive teaching — `Foe: unrecorded species`

This gambit subject selects a living foe whose species is absent from the bestiary. It pairs with the
existing **Read** skill: “Collecting. A bestiary entry without a kill.” A recruited Kestrel can enable:

> **Foe: unrecorded species → Read**

Once Read records that species, the condition stops matching it. This is useful, legible and deeply
specific to Kestrel without giving her a duplicated world focus. It restores a player choice: learn
first, then decide whether and how to fight.

Later combat design may add `Foe: known weakness`, but it should wait until attacks have meaningful
damage or emanation types to choose between.

### Coherence sentence

> Because Kestrel learned to **recognise a creature before reducing it to threat or material**, she
> can teach the party to identify an **unrecorded species** in combat and recover more from anatomy
> they genuinely understand.

## Signature and diary shape

Her five conditions should form a complete hunting ecology: prey evidence, predator pressure,
traversable cover, a reliable resource route and one environmental factor that changes behaviour.

**Initial target: 9 pages**

| Pages | Purpose |
|---:|---|
| 5 | One location or creature-behaviour page per signature condition |
| 1 | `Foe: unrecorded species` teaching |
| 1 | Sabine disagreement: studying an animal to take it versus to live beside it |
| 1 | Fen relationship page: maker and user disputing what a clean shot proves |
| 1 | Waste site, mistaken identification or Kestrel whereabouts page |

### Diary arc

- **First impression:** Kestrel's certainty appears to come from exceptional instincts.
- **Complication:** An identification was correct, but her assumption about the creature's situation
  was not; efficient action still caused avoidable harm.
- **After recruitment:** She remains a hunter but becomes more exact about the boundary between what
  a trace proves and what she has inferred.

## Relationship anchors

- **Sabine:** The central care/hunt disagreement. Neither should be made naïve so the other can win.
- **Fen:** Fen prepares the trajectory; Kestrel decides whether releasing it is warranted.
- **Lys:** Field notes become falsely authoritative after their uncertainty is edited away.
- **Bracken:** Material recovery from bodies versus remembering what the material cost.

# Lys — Archivist

## Identity

| Field | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Pronouns** | she/her |
| **Calling** | an archivist |
| **Building** | The Library |
| **Campaign phase** | Mid |
| **Signature complexity** | 4 conditions |
| **Teaching** | **Echo**, diary-exclusive |
| **Starting lean** | Ingenuity + Precision |

### One-line blurb

> Keeps contradictory accounts beside each other. Says agreement is useful, but absence is evidence.

### Voice anchors

- **Habit:** Cross-references margins, changes of hand, repeated phrasing and conspicuous omissions.
  She preserves competing versions instead of silently synthesising them.
- **Attitude:** Patient, incisive and wary of a neat story arriving before enough fragments do.
- **Tension:** An archive can preserve a person's words long after they could correct them. Context
  protects records from misuse, but no amount of context restores the speaker's control.

Lys is a person with an archival practice, not the Library interface in human form. Her authored
concern is the power involved in deciding which relationships between fragments become visible.

## Why the player seeks her

- keeper-led Library depth that makes the expanding diary corpus searchable and legible;
- cross-references between people, worlds, places and focuses;
- deeper hint-page interpretation without directly solving signatures;
- visible contradictions, alternate accounts and unresolved references;
- rune study grounded in the records the player has actually found.

### Coherence sentence

> Because Lys preserves **relationships and contradictions between fragments**, she can make the
> player's growing page corpus legible without pretending it forms one complete account, which is
> why recruiting her gives the existing **Library cross-references and deeper page study**.

## Echo

Echo is a record's central problem: repetition makes something present again, but distance and its
surroundings change what returns. Lys's teaching page should compare two nearly identical accounts
whose differences matter more than their shared wording.

Echo is Lys's diary-exclusive focus; its former ordinary late-wild route is superseded.

## Signature and diary shape

Her four-condition world should repeat a recognisable structure, sound, ecology or route with one
meaningful variation each time. The player learns to notice relationships, not find four copies.

**Initial target: 9 pages**

| Pages | Purpose |
|---:|---|
| 4 | One location page per signature condition |
| 1 | Echo teaching |
| 1 | Two conflicting accounts preserved together |
| 1 | Vance, Isolde or Edren relationship page |
| 1 | A record Lys chose not to catalogue |
| 1 | Library precursor or Lys whereabouts page |

### Diary arc

- **First impression:** Lys appears to distrust conclusions because she is more rigorous than others.
- **Complication:** She once preserved a harmful account in the name of completeness without giving
  equal care to the living person it continued to define.
- **After recruitment:** She still refuses false consensus, but treats access, arrangement and
  emphasis as consequential editorial acts rather than neutral procedure.

## Relationship anchors

- **Vance:** Circulation preserves use; archives preserve relation. Both can withhold provenance.
- **Kestrel:** A field inference gains false certainty when filed as a fact.
- **Isolde:** Memory as lived continuity versus the external record that survives it.
- **Edren:** Classification reveals patterns but can erase exceptions as noise.

# Ashe — Emanant

## Identity

| Field | Recommendation |
|---|---|
| **Pronouns** | they/them |
| **Calling** | an Emanant: a person whose body carries and expresses emanation |
| **Cast type** | Dedicated fighter |
| **Campaign phase** | Late |
| **Signature complexity** | 8 conditions |
| **Teaching** | Exclusive gambit subject: **Foe: emanating** |
| **Starting lean** | Force + Fortitude, with Kindling access |

### What an Emanant is

An **Emanant** is a person in whom emanation persists without a crafted housing. Ashe's own emanation
is specifically **thermal**, while their embodied sensitivity lets them recognise other forms of
emanation. Their body is not a
weapon-shaped container: sensation, regulation, fatigue and expression are inseparable from the
phenomenon. Oda builds channels that keep emanation bounded and directed. Ashe lives with boundaries
that breathe, scar, tire and choose.

This should be rare and authored, not a new generic species or spellcasting class. It establishes one
person's condition without implying that anybody can learn generic elemental magic.

### One-line blurb

> Feels emanation before an instrument can name it. Has spent too long being thanked for enduring
> what nobody asked whether they wished to carry.

### Voice anchors

- **Habit:** Describes emanation as bodily sensations—pressure behind the teeth, heat without warmth,
  a rhythm in the joints—rather than abstract elements.
- **Attitude:** Direct and dryly observant. They distinguish discomfort, danger and loss of control;
  other people tend to collapse all three into panic.
- **Tension:** Ashe wants their ability taken seriously without being treated as public equipment.
  Refusing to help can protect their autonomy while leaving others exposed to a danger they alone
  recognised.

## Why the player seeks them

- earlier recognition of emanating and warded foes;
- a combat vocabulary for when an enemy is actively projecting or containing emanation;
- techniques that receive, ground or redirect elemental pressure at a bodily cost;
- a late-game fighter whose strength creates resource and timing decisions rather than free damage.

### Recommended exclusive teaching — `Foe: emanating`

This gambit subject matches a foe currently projecting, charged with or maintained by emanation. It
supports rules such as:

> **Foe: emanating → Ward**  
> **Foe: emanating → Snuff**

It gives the player reliable automation against elemental threats without inventing a second Echo
owner or requiring a new world focus. Exact detection timing belongs in the combat implementation
spec.

Ashe also arrives with **Ground**, a protective active technique that receives the next emanation
event aimed at another party member, halves it, and attempts its affliction against Ashe. It is part
of Ashe's kit rather than another exclusive teaching; see `ashe-ground-technique-current.md`.

### Coherence sentence

> Because Ashe experiences **emanation as an embodied state rather than an external effect**, they
> can recognise when a foe is actively **emanating** and help the party contain or redirect it, at a
> cost that remains theirs to consent to.

## Signature and diary shape

Ashe's eight-condition world should contain several distinct emanation pressures interacting through
terrain, weather and life. It must not simply be “all elements at once.”

**Initial target: 12 pages**

| Pages | Purpose |
|---:|---|
| 8 | One location or bodily-perception page per signature condition |
| 1 | `Foe: emanating` teaching |
| 1 | Oda relationship page: housing problem versus autonomous person |
| 1 | A prior use of Ashe's ability that was praised but not freely chosen |
| 1 | Containment site or Ashe whereabouts page |

### Diary arc

- **First impression:** Ashe appears uniquely capable of enduring emanation that would overwhelm
  anyone else.
- **Complication:** Endurance became an expectation, and their accurate warnings were ignored until
  they agreed to personally absorb the consequence.
- **After recruitment:** They do not become unwilling to help. Recruitment establishes that help can
  be negotiated, limited and refused without denying the reality of their strength.

## Relationship anchors

- **Oda:** Oda initially reads Ashe through containment practice; Ashe forces her to confront the
  difference between a housing and a person.
- **Tovin:** Instruments produce useful readings while failing to describe lived sensation.
- **Nine:** Both are treated as evidence of forces larger than themselves and resist becoming an
  explanation instead of a person.
- **Bryn:** Protection offered freely can still become an identity other people enforce.

## Naming decision

**Ashe** is retained. The name's association with heat and cinders is intentional because their own
emanation is thermal. Their broader combat usefulness comes from recognising other emanations, not
from personally embodying every element.
